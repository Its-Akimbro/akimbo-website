### Inferences
- In the Triad, a free assessment is the norm. Every Triad firm I found that describes an assessment either gives it away or hides the price. A published fixed price would be unusual locally. That could make Akimbo's offer stand out, but prospects will compare it with "free."
- Local free assessments are typically short (30-60 min), on-site or call-based, and produce a summary or color-coded report plus a proposal (RTG, Trinity). Akimbo's paid offer needs to be visibly deeper to justify a fee: a full device inventory, a written risk report, a walk-through, and a report the client can take to any provider.
- Local hourly rates (about $125-$150/hr standard) imply that a one-day on-site audit plus report-writing (about 8-12 billable hours) would cost about $1,000-$1,800 at local rack rates. This is my calculation from cited rates, not a published price.

## Q2. What are national/US typical price ranges for small-business network audits, how do they scale with devices/sites/users, and what scope limits do sellers attach?

### Takeaway
Nationally, published paid prices for a single-site small-business network audit cluster at **$750-$3,500**. Vendor guides put single offices with fewer than 25 endpoints at **$1,500-$3,000**, 25-75 endpoints at **$3,000-$5,000**, and multi-site 75-200 endpoints at **$5,000-$7,500**. Security-heavy or compliance audits run $3,000-$15,000+. Sellers scope by sites, computers/endpoints, users, servers and network devices, and often cap on-site time at a half day to two days.

## Q3. What deliverables typically come with paid vs free assessments?

### Takeaway
Free assessments are usually a 30-60-minute call or scan that yields a short summary (top risks or a color-coded report) plus a sales proposal. Paid assessments add a full device inventory, network diagram, risk-ranked findings with severity and cost estimates, a remediation roadmap, and a live walkthrough or debrief. The client usually keeps the report regardless of who does the fixes.

### Cited Findings
- **Free (Triad), RTG Solutions:** 30-60 min on-site review; color-coded green/yellow/red summary; ~15-min follow-up; resolution proposal with options; portable to other providers. — [RTG Solutions](https://rtgsolutions.tech/how-it-works/)
- **Free (Triad), Trinity Solutions:** plain-English report on "where you stand", gap identification and estimated cost to close. A separate 30-minute cybersecurity assessment comes with no written report described. — [Trinity Solutions](https://trinitysolutionsinc.com/managed-it-services-in-winston-salem/)
- **Free (national), Xonicwave** (San Diego; titled "FREE Network Assessment – $2,499 Value"): 30-minute phone/video review delivering the top 3 risks ranked, plain-English next steps, an honest "do you need help" answer and a written copy. "High-level review, not a penetration test or compliance audit"; "won't find every issue". One per business per 12 months. — [Xonicwave](https://xonicwave.com/free-network-assessment)
- **Free (national), LDI** (Jericho, NY; "normally a $1,495 value"): about 30-minute network scan, 6-8 page executive summary, network risk score, issue analysis and security suggestions. — [LDI Network Assessment](https://myldi.com/network-assessment/)
- **Free (national), Agility Networks** (Chicago; page about 2 years old): "Free Network Evaluation includes a technical look into one local network". Requires admin credentials. Delivers an executive summary, risks, remediation steps and a presentation of findings. "No Fee in Most Cases within Agility Services Area". — [Agility Networks](https://agilitynetworks.com/it-services-projects/it-audits-and-assessments/)

- **Paid (Charlotte), NetSafe Solutions:** written report with diagrams, photos, config screenshots and performance test results; severity-tagged findings; prioritized remediation with cost estimates; plain-language executive summary; closeout call. Client keeps the report. — [NetSafe](https://netsafesolutions.com/network-assessment-services)

- **Cavelo** (assessment-tool vendor, Nov 19, 2026): the entry assessment typically covers asset inventory and unmanaged devices, vulnerabilities and missing patches, sensitive-data discovery, stale or excessive access, and a business-focused risk summary. — [Security Boulevard / Cavelo](https://securityboulevard.com/2026/11/the-complete-guide-to-msp-assessment-recurring-revenue-cavelo/)

### Inferences

## Q4. Is it common to credit the audit fee against a subsequent managed-services contract or project? Examples and terms.

### Takeaway
Crediting is described as common by an assessment-tool vendor, but I found only one published, verifiable example with explicit terms: Isofinite credits a $1,495 fee toward the first 3 months of a plan if the client signs within 90 days. ### Inferences
- A credit-back clause (e.g., the fee credited against the first 1-3 months of managed services, or against a project, if signed within 60-90 days) is a reasonable way to soften a paid price in a market where competitors give assessments away. The Isofinite wording is a ready template.
- Not crediting (the Fusion approach) supports an "independent, no-obligation" positioning. Akimbo could choose either. The two approaches send different messages (sales funnel vs. independent advice).

### Gaps
- I found no survey data on what share of MSPs credit assessment fees, and no Triad or NC example of a published credit policy. The IT-Charlotte credit claim could not be verified.

## Q5. How do Triad prices compare to Charlotte, Raleigh-Durham, and national averages?

### Takeaway
No Triad firm publishes an assessment price, so I could not compare prices directly. Charlotte has the only published NC price points ($299-$599 for a 5-15-device security assessment; IT audit "from $500"). Raleigh firms quote $3,000-$15,000 for professional security assessments. National single-office ranges are $1,500-$3,000 (fewer than 25 endpoints). Triad managed-IT and hourly rates look similar to or slightly below Charlotte, which fits the 15-30% "smaller market" discount cited nationally.

National single-office benchmarks ($750-$3,500) and local hourly rates ($125-$150/hr) point to roughly **$1,000-$2,000** as a defensible Triad price for one site with a modest device cap. This is my synthesis, not a published figure.
