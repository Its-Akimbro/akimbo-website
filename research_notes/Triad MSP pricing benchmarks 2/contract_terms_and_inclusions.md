## 1. Contract length, auto-renewal, notice periods and early termination fees

### Takeaway
Nationally, the default contract still runs 1-3 years, renews automatically, needs 60 days' notice before the end of the term, and charges about 50% of the remaining fees to leave early. That said, 38% of MSPs in the one survey offer monthly contracts. In the Triad, "no long-term contract" claims are common in marketing: Trinity Solutions, ComTech, ITECH2, and several out-of-area firms with Triad landing pages all make them. Not one Triad firm I found publishes a notice period or early termination fee rule. Month-to-month alone is therefore not a differentiator here. Publishing the exact mechanics would be: notice period, no early termination fee, no renewal into a fixed term, and no seat-count floor.

### Inferences
- In the Triad, "month-to-month" is now a commodity claim: Trinity, ComTech, ITECH2, SpectrumWise, Insight and Winston IT all make it. But none of them publish the exact terms. Akimbo can go further by publishing all of them on the plans page:
  - notice period (30 days, to the end of the billing month)
  - no early termination fee
  - no auto-renewal into a new fixed term
  - no minimum seat or device count
  - a price-change notice of at least 30 days, with a right to leave
- The Scott & Scott template is likely what many local MSP contracts look like, since it is sold to MSPs. Its clauses are exactly what a "no hostage-taking" page can name and promise against. They are a 60-day renewal notice in capitals, a 50% early termination fee, a seat-count floor and renewal on "then-current terms".
- If Akimbo needs to cover onboarding costs without lock-in, local precedent exists. Net Friends charges a one-time onboarding fee of about one month's service. Akimbo could do the same, or charge for the paid audit as a separate engagement.
- Trinity and Dynamic Quest both bill a month in advance. Billing monthly in advance with 30 days' notice is standard and low-risk for a solo operator.

### Gaps
- No Triad firm publishes an MSA with a notice period or early termination fee. Dynamic Quest's MSA body is not public, and only its addenda are.
- No survey gives the share of IT MSP contracts at each length (month-to-month, 12, 24, 36 months). MSP Success gives "% of MSPs offering" each length, which is a different measure, and it reports no sample size.
- r/msp threads were not reachable because the domain is blocked to this crawler.
- ComTech's post-acquisition terms (Harbor IT) are unverified.

## 2. Support hours, after-hours rates and response-time SLAs

### Takeaway
Small, local, founder-run MSPs typically staff support during business hours (8-5 or 9-5, Monday to Friday) and add 24/7 automated monitoring. After-hours help is limited to critical outages or sold as an add-on. Larger shops advertise a 24x7x365 help desk: Dynamic Quest (100+ staff), Net Friends, and SpectrumWise. The most detailed local SLA is Dynamic Quest's. It sets response objectives of 30 minutes for critical, 90 minutes for high and 4 business hours for medium. These are objectives, not guarantees. The national convention for after-hours work is 1.5x the normal rate, 2x for holidays or emergencies, with a 1-hour minimum.

### Inferences
- **What's realistic for a solo founder:** published business hours (Monday to Friday 8-5 or 8-6 ET), 24/7 automated monitoring that alerts the founder, and best-effort after-hours response for critical outages. Akimbo's peers on that model are Bonelli ("no 24/7 IT help desk") and the small-business tier at Winston IT. A solo operator should not copy the 15-30 minute guaranteed critical responses from Net Friends, Trinity or Dynamic Quest, which are multi-staff shops. NOCDOC calls a single on-call tech a "breach risk".
- **Infrastructure-only fit:** Dynamic Quest's severity model maps well onto infrastructure-only scope, defining severity by share of users affected (majority / 10-49% / 5-10% / 1-5% / 1-2 users). Akimbo could use 3 levels, for example: site or server down; degraded or partial; and single request or change.
- **Honest SLA wording:** state response objectives on a business-hours clock, and say plainly that they are targets, not resolution guarantees. Dynamic Quest ("Objective"), IT Solutions and Bonelli all do this. One illustrative set a solo operator can defend:
  - Critical: 1 business hour, with best-effort after-hours response.
  - High: 4 business hours.
  - Normal: next business day.
- **Tier lever:** after-hours coverage is the conventional difference between tiers. For example:
  - Essentials: after-hours billed at 1.5x (2x on holidays), 1-hour minimum.
  - Managed: after-hours critical outages on covered network gear included.
  - Managed + Servers: after-hours critical outages on servers and network included.

  This matches the N-able ladder, Winston IT, and E-N Computers ("critical alerts and work stoppage at no extra charge").
- **Clock wording matters:** a "4-hour" target means different things on a 24/7 clock and a business-hours clock, so Akimbo should state which clock applies.

### Gaps

- I could not verify whether Trinity's "15 minutes" means an automated acknowledgement or a technician engaging.
- Dynamic Quest's Addendum D refers to a "Management Escalation Levels" table (Table 2) that did not extract from the PDF, probably because it is an image.
- I found no survey data on the share of small MSPs offering 24/7 live support versus business hours only.

## 3. On-site visits: allowances, trip charges, service radius

### Takeaway
Locally, the norm is on-site support included with no trip charge, often marketed as "unlimited". Trinity, Petronella and ITECH2 all say so, and Trinity says outright "We never charge trip fees". The exception is IDeACOM, which reserves "on-site hours" for its top tier. National contracts usually leave the visit count to the order form, set a radius (for example Net Friends' 50 miles), or pass travel costs through. Remote-first wording is universal: on-site only when the problem can't be fixed remotely. Charging trip fees inside the Triad would put Akimbo below the local norm. A radius plus "on-site when remote can't resolve it" matches what peers actually promise.

### Inferences
- **Recommended on-site model:** for Akimbo, the locally competitive and solo-sustainable approach is "on-site included, when remote can't resolve it, for covered infrastructure, within the Triad (or X miles of Winston-Salem/Greensboro). No trip charge inside that area; mileage beyond." This beats Piedmont Technical Experts and the HomeAdvisor-style trip fees, and matches Trinity and Net Friends without promising "unlimited".
- **Scheduled visits as a tier lever:** E-N Computers' "focus day" and IDeACOM's "on-site hours" show that scheduled visits work as a tier lever. For example, Managed + Servers could include a quarterly on-site review or maintenance visit. Lower tiers would get incident-driven on-site visits only.
- **Visit cancellation:** a 24-48 hour cancellation rule for scheduled visits, as in Dynamic Quest's, is standard and protects a solo founder's calendar.
- **Positioning against Trinity:** Trinity's "same day" on-site promise is the local benchmark. A solo founder would do better to promise "next business day on-site for critical issues, same day when available" than to match "same day".

## 4. Offboarding and exit terms (documentation, credential handover, fees)

### Takeaway
No Triad MSP I found publishes an offboarding or handover commitment. At Dynamic Quest, the region's largest MSP, customers get access to their own IT Glue documentation only as a paid monthly add-on, usually read-only, and DQ "has the right to … refuse". The MSP-side template default (Scott & Scott) is the "hostage" model Akimbo's messaging pushes against:
- the MSP holds exclusive admin credentials;
- handover is billed at then-prevailing rates;
- help is conditional on paying any termination fee;
- copies of client data cost a "data-copy fee";
- data is deleted 30 days after exit.

Buyer guides now tell customers to demand handover of "all documentation, credentials, backups and configurations" within 30-60 days. A free, published exit clause would be clearly differentiating in the Triad.

  - client-held "break-glass" admin accounts, the opposite of the Scott & Scott "exclusive administrative credentials" standard;
  - a published exit package.
- Example exit package wording that answers buyer-guide demands while staying sustainable for a solo founder:
  - Full documentation, configs, credentials and backups delivered within 10 business days of notice, at no charge.
  - Up to 2 hours of handover call with the incoming provider included; further help at the standard hourly rate.
  - No help conditional on paying an early termination fee (there is none).
  - Agents removed on the exit date.
  - Backups kept N days after exit for the client to retrieve.
- Giant Rocketship's MSP-side view (basic handover in the recurring fee, extras billed) means a free basic handover is not a commercial risk. It is what fair MSPs already consider proper.

### Gaps

- Dynamic Quest's MSA body, which may contain exit terms, is not public.
- No Triad terms page addresses data return timing or credential handover.

## 5. How small-business MSP tier tables are structured (good/better/best)

### Takeaway
Most MSPs (71% in the MSP Success survey) offer two or three tiers, and a quarter offer none. The usual ladder is cumulative ("Tier N = Tier N-1 + …"). It moves from monitoring and patching to security and backup, then to strategic work (vCIO/QBRs), on-site time and after-hours coverage. Contract terms are not usually a tier lever. Support hours, response targets, on-site inclusion and number of servers covered are. Winston IT's ladder (business hours with one server, then extended hours with multiple servers, then a 24/7 add-on) is the closest analogue to Akimbo's scope-based Essentials, Managed and Managed + Servers.

### Inferences
- **Same in every tier, to back "no hostage-taking":** Akimbo's tiers are defined by scope, which is unusual locally (IDeACOM's ladder is security-led). Rows to keep identical across tiers: contract (month-to-month, 30 days' notice, no early termination fee), documentation and credential ownership, exit package, and 24/7 monitoring. Making these upsells would undercut the message, and Dynamic Quest's paid documentation add-on is the counter-example.
- **Conventional rows to vary by tier:**
  - **Scope:** network only, then network plus managed services, then servers and virtualisation.
  - **After-hours critical response:** billed in the lowest tier, included in the upper tiers.
  - **Response objective:** for example next business day, then 4 business hours, then 1 business hour for critical.
  - **On-site:** billed hourly, then included for covered incidents, then included plus scheduled quarterly visits.
  - **Reviews:** annual, then quarterly.
- **Akimbo-specific table rows:** a "What happens if you leave" row and a "Your documentation" row. Neither appears in any Triad competitor's tables. They make the differentiator visible in the place buyers compare plans.
- **Winston IT precedent:** its "up to 1 server" limit in the base tier shows that a server-count boundary between tiers is an accepted way to price server work separately. That validates Akimbo's "Managed + Servers" split.

### Gaps
- No Triad MSP publishes a full tier table with response times or on-site allowances per tier. IDeACOM lists inclusions only, and Winston IT's location is unverified.
- Dynamic Quest's bundle PDFs (May 2026) returned an HTML client-hub page instead of a PDF, so their contents are unknown.
- I found no data on which tier buyers choose most often, or on typical price gaps between tiers (pricing is out of scope here anyway).
