# Hourly and Project Rates for IT/Network Work, and the MSP Landscape, in the Piedmont Triad (Winston-Salem / Greensboro NC)

Research date: 2026-11-24. Labels: **[LOCAL]** = Winston-Salem/Greensboro/Triad; **[NC]** = other North Carolina; **[NATIONAL]** or a named region = outside the Triad. "Search summary" means the figure came from a search-engine result summary and the page itself was not fetched, so treat it as less certain than figures marked as fetched.

## 1. Typical hourly rates in the Triad (on-site tech, network engineer, remote, after-hours, trip fees) vs national and Charlotte/Raleigh

### Takeaway
Published Triad rates fall into two tiers. Solo and consumer break-fix shops post $50-$95/hr on-site, about $60/hr remote, and $125/hr after hours. Business-focused MSPs in the region and across NC rarely publish rates, but the evidence that exists puts them at about $125-$200/hr. National MSP averages are about $100-$149/hr (Clutch) and $109.79/hr (2023 survey of 414 MSPs). No Triad firm was found publishing a separate "network engineer" rate.

### Inferences
- **Two Triad price tiers.** The "consumer/solo" tier is $50-$95/hr on-site and $60/hr remote. The "business IT" tier is about $125-$200/hr: DesignRush's $125 local average, $150 in the Raleigh listing, $100-$199 in Charlotte Clutch listings, and $200 from HomeAdvisor's national provider. A founder-led infrastructure specialist billing $125-$165/hr for project and out-of-plan work would sit inside the business tier and near or slightly above the national MSP average ($109.79 in 2023; $100-$149 on Clutch).
- **After-hours premium.** The only local after-hours figure is Great Scott's $125/hr against a $95/hr business rate, a premium of about 1.3x.
- **Trip fees.** The only explicit trip fee found is $100 (HomeAdvisor's national provider). Local small shops instead charge $40 diagnosis fees or no trip fee within the county. CyberITEX in Charlotte advertises "no trip charge".
- **Charlotte and Raleigh.** Published rates are similar to or somewhat higher than the Triad business tier ($100-$199/hr; $150 Raleigh add-on support). BLS wages (Q3) support a modest Raleigh premium for sysadmins but not a clear Charlotte premium.

### Gaps
- No Triad MSP or infrastructure firm was found publishing a "network engineer" or "senior engineer" hourly rate, and none of the 17 Greensboro MSPs on Jumpfactor's list publishes rates. Mystery-shopping or quote requests would be needed.
- No local source gave after-hours or emergency multipliers beyond Great Scott, and no local business-MSP trip or dispatch fee was found.
- I found r/msp and r/sysadmin rate threads, but none was fetched or quoted, so this note contains no practitioner forum data.
- A 2016 NCDIT short-term IT staffing solicitation (commodity 918) reportedly lists not-to-exceed hourly rates such as $74.18 and $100.89. I could not tie these to a verified URL, and they are a decade old, so they are excluded. The current NCDIT 920S IT Services contract rate cards were not retrieved.

## 2. Typical project pricing for small-business network work (firewall, switch/Wi-Fi refresh per AP, cabling per drop, server migration/virtualisation, block-hour discounts)

### Takeaway
No Triad-specific project price lists were found. The best available anchors, all national or from other regions:
- Cat6 drops cost $125-$300 each.
- An installed Wi-Fi access point costs $650-$1,400 all-in.
- A managed-firewall install costs about $1,400-$2,000 one-time, with complete firewall replacement projects quoted at $3,000-$15,000.
- SMB hypervisor or server migration labor runs $3,000-$15,000, excluding hardware and licences.
- Block-hour prepay discounts are small, about 3-8%.

NC state government marks up cabling vendor cost by 11.5% and charges $500 per site for a LAN install.

### Inferences
- **Block-hour discount.** The 2023 survey's block-hour average is about 4% below its hourly average ($105.18 vs $109.79), consistent with Connecting Point's 3-8%. A 5-10% block discount would be at or above market norms.
- **Cabling.** The NY-sourced Cat6 range ($150-$250) likely overstates Triad prices, given Triad wages near or below the US level (Q3). Fixr's $125-$250 is a reasonable band for local drops.
- **Wi-Fi.** A per-AP-installed price of roughly $650-$1,400 (hardware + mount + config + drop) aligns with the national benchmark. Using NCDIT's $500-per-site LAN install as a floor for a site visit is defensible.
- **Firewall.** VC3's one-time install fee ($1,400-$2,000, 2022) suggests labour-only firewall replacement quotes of about 8-16 hours at $125/hr. This is arithmetic, not a sourced figure.

### Gaps
- No Triad-sourced per-drop, per-AP, firewall or server-migration quotes were found. Local cabling contractors (e.g., Triad Network Systems, Integrated Networks; see Q4) do not publish prices.
- No server-virtualisation project price from a US MSP was found. The only figure is a non-US vendor estimate.
- No HomeGuide/Angi pages were found for NC business network work, nor any Greensboro or Winston-Salem pages for these project types.

## 3. BLS OEWS wages: network/computer systems administrators and computer support specialists, Winston-Salem MSA and Greensboro-High Point MSA vs US (and Charlotte/Raleigh)

### Takeaway
May 2025 OEWS data (latest; published 2026) shows Triad IT wages near or modestly below the US level:
- **Sysadmins:** the Winston-Salem median is $45.46/hr (95% of the US $47.66) and the Greensboro-High Point median is $42.59/hr (89% of US).
- **User-support specialists:** the median is $28.37 in Winston-Salem and $26.65 in Greensboro-High Point, vs $29.74 US.

Raleigh pays sysadmins more ($49.70 median). Charlotte's sysadmin median ($43.26) is about the Triad level, but Charlotte network architects earn much more ($64.62 median).

### Inferences
- **Ratio to US medians.** Winston-Salem runs about 93-95% of US medians for sysadmin, network-support and user-support roles. Greensboro-High Point runs about 89-90%. Network architects run 89% (Winston-Salem) and 85% (Greensboro-High Point).
- **Raleigh and Charlotte.** Raleigh sysadmins earn about 9% more than Winston-Salem and about 17% more than Greensboro-High Point at the median. Charlotte network architects earn about 12-17% more than Triad architects. Charlotte sysadmins do not earn more than Triad sysadmins.
- **Wage growth.** The Winston-Salem sysadmin median rose about 12% from May 2023 ($40.56) to May 2025 ($45.46), which supports annual rate increases.
- **Bill-rate multiples (arithmetic).** A $125/hr bill rate is 2.75x the Winston-Salem sysadmin median wage ($45.46) and 2.9x the Greensboro-High Point median ($42.59). $150/hr is 3.3x and 3.5x respectively. Local solo shops' $95/hr is 3.3x the Winston-Salem user-support median ($28.37).
- **Cabling labour.** Telecom installer wages of about $30-$33/hr suggest Triad cabling labour costs no more than the US average.

### Gaps
- Employment counts and percentiles for Charlotte, Raleigh and US rows were not retrieved, only means and medians.
- Benefits/overhead burden multipliers (e.g., BLS Employer Costs for Employee Compensation) were not researched, so a fully loaded labour cost is not sourced here.
- MSA definitions changed with OMB's 2023 delineations, so whether May 2025 OEWS uses the new boundaries was not verified.

## 4. Main MSPs/IT providers serving Triad small businesses; infrastructure-only specialists; local small-business market size (10-100 employee businesses in Forsyth/Guilford)

### Takeaway
Forsyth + Guilford had 6,600 establishments with 10-99 employees in 2023 (Census CBP), and the full two-MSA Triad had about 8,800. The local IT-services supply is fragmented: 310 NAICS 54151 establishments in the two counties, about 70% with under 5 employees.

The competitive set is mostly full-service MSPs, none publishing prices:
- Dynamic Quest (PE-backed, acquisitive, mid-market).
- Franchises (CMIT Solutions, TeamLogic IT).
- Long-tenured independents (ComTech, Total Computer Solutions, Trinity Solutions, Cypress Networks).
- Out-of-market firms with Triad SEO pages (Net Friends, Petronella, Mindcore).

No pure "infrastructure-only MSP" was found. The closest analogues are network-heavy MSPs (Cypress Networks) and cabling/low-voltage integrators (Integrated Networks, Triad Network Systems, Instrata).

### Inferences
- **Market size.** The 6,600 Forsyth + Guilford establishments with 10-99 employees (about 8,800 across both MSAs) is an upper bound on Akimbo's addressable market. CBP counts establishments, not firms, so branches of chains, banks and national companies are included and usually buy IT centrally. The 100-employee boundary falls inside the 100-249 class, so "10-100 staff" is approximated as 10-99.
- **Price opacity.** No local business-focused MSP publishes hourly or project rates, so a published project-rate card or "starting at" anchors would differentiate Akimbo. The only firms that publish prices are solo break-fix shops, whose $50-$95/hr rates prospects may use as a comparison point.
- **Infrastructure-only gap.** Full-service MSPs (help desk + security + cloud) dominate, and cabling integrators do physical infrastructure but not managed networking. An "infrastructure-only managed networking + projects" positioning appears unoccupied by any firm found. It could also be pitched as a co-managed or subcontract partner to the many 1-5 person IT shops (70% of the 310 local NAICS 54151 establishments), as Great Scott does with "smart hands".
- **Consolidation.** Dynamic Quest's PE backing and serial acquisitions signal consolidation of the local MSP market, typically toward larger clients.

### Gaps
- Triad Business Journal's "largest IT firms" or Book of Lists ranking was not retrievable (paywalled or not in results), so authoritative local headcounts for Dynamic Quest, Total Computer Solutions, ComTech, Trinity and others are missing.
- CloudTango's Winston-Salem MSP directory returned HTTP 403.
- I found no Clutch or Google review counts.
- No data was found on how many Triad 10-99-employee businesses already use an MSP, or on Forsyth/Guilford business counts by firm (rather than establishment).
