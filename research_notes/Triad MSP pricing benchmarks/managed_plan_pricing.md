# Monthly managed-services pricing for an infrastructure-only MSP (Piedmont Triad focus)

Research date: 2026-11-24. "Fetched" means the page itself was read. "Snippet" means the figure came only from a search-result summary and was not confirmed on the page, so treat it as weaker.

## 1. Which Triad-area MSPs publish prices, and what share hide pricing behind a quote?

### Takeaway
I found no Triad-based MSP that publishes firm list prices. Firms either give a typical per-user range in an FAQ or blog post, or say "starting at a per-seat rate" with no dollar figure and send buyers to an assessment. The local ranges they cite for full managed IT (help desk included) cluster around **$100-$300 per user per month**, or **about $2,000-$6,000 per month for a 20-30 person office**.

### Cited Findings
- **Trinity Solutions** (1224 Eastchester Dr, High Point; serves Winston-Salem and Greensboro). The FAQ says managed IT "typically ranges" **$100-$300/user/month**, about **$2,000-$5,000/month for a 20-person office**. Plans are custom-built after a free assessment, with no network, server or per-device prices. It advertises month-to-month contracts and no trip charges. Fetched. — [Trinity Solutions, Winston-Salem page](https://trinitysolutionsinc.com/managed-it-services-in-winston-salem/); also [Greensboro page](https://trinitysolutionsinc.com/managed-it-services-in-greensboro/)
- **Petronella Technology Group** (HQ Raleigh, with Winston-Salem and Greensboro landing pages; not truly Triad-based). It has three per-seat tiers with **no dollar amounts**:

  - Business, 25-100 users, and Enterprise, 100-250 users: "Custom per-seat rate".
  - A firm quote comes after a scope call.
  - Its FAQ says servers, specialty devices and cloud-only users "may be counted differently" from workstations, and the counting method is set during discovery.

- Petronella's Winston-Salem page says plans start at "a flat monthly rate per user or device", with a detailed quote after a free assessment. Snippet. — [Petronella Winston-Salem](https://petronellatech.com/managed-it-services-winston-salem-nc)

### Inferences
- Of the Triad-serving firms I looked at (Trinity, Petronella, First Column, plus aggregator pages), **none publish a firm "from $X" plan price**. That is effectively 100% quote-only, with at most a ranges FAQ.
  - The sample is small, about 3-4 firms.
  - Even so, a published "from" price would be unusual locally and would stand out.
- All local figures are for **full-stack, per-user managed IT that includes help desk**. An infrastructure-only offer should sit well below $100/user, because desktop and help-desk support is the biggest cost driver in those bundles.

### Gaps
- I did not check every Triad MSP site. Firms such as First Column and other Winston-Salem and Greensboro shops were not fetched, and the share that publish prices rests on a small sample.
- I found no Triad MSP that sells a network-only or server-only plan with a published price.

## 2. Typical national rates: full managed IT, and infrastructure or network-only device rates (2024-2026)

### Takeaway
- **National full managed IT** runs about **$100-$250/user/month**. Basic tiers start around $50-$110, and security-inclusive bundles run $200-$275+.
- **Infrastructure line items** I found:
  - Firewall: about **$50-$150+/month**.
  - Switch: about **$45/month**.
  - Access point: about **$10-$50/month**.
  - Server: about **$100-$400/month** (an older Kaseya average is **$156**; the best published 2026 list price is **$149**).
  - Whole site, network bundled: about **$50-$650/month** depending on complexity.

### Cited Findings: full managed IT per user (national)

This is the most recent Kaseya per-user figure I found; the 2025 report's pricing figures are behind a form. Snippet. — [Kaseya MSP pricing guide](https://www.kaseya.com/?p=15042); [Kaseya 2025 Benchmark key findings](https://www.kaseya.com/blog/key-findings-from-kaseyas-2025-global-msp-benchmark-report/)
- **N-able** (an RMM vendor) puts standard managed services at **$110-$175/user/month** and security-inclusive bundles at **$200-$275**. Snippet. — [N-able MSP pricing guide](https://www.n-able.com/blog/msp-pricing-guide)
- **Flamingo** gives **$70-$150/user/month** for 1-50 seats (2026). Snippet. — [Flamingo MSP pricing models (May 2026)](https://www.flamingo.run/blog/msp-pricing-models)
- **Huntress** says growing businesses typically pay **$100-$250/user/month**. Snippet. — [Huntress MSP pricing guide](https://www.huntress.com/msp-guide/building-your-msp-pricing-model)
- **EasyITGuys** (St Croix Falls, WI; Sept 30, 2025) gives these per-user tiers. Fetched. — [EasyITGuys pricing deep dive](https://easyitguys.com/pricing/part-3-it-pricing-deep-dive/)

### Gaps
- I found no published per-hypervisor-host or per-VM price from a commercial MSP.
- The current Kaseya, ConnectWise and Datto benchmark medians are behind forms, so I could not extract them.

## 3. What pricing model fits a network-plus-servers-only MSP, and what do comparable infrastructure-focused MSPs charge?

### Takeaway
The clearest comparables price infrastructure **per site/location (network bundled) plus per server**, not per user. Their reasoning is that network gear scales with sites and complexity, not headcount. The best published analog is Micro-IT:

| Item | Price per month |
|---|---|
| Managed site, small | $149 |
| Managed site, standard | $329 |
| Managed site, complex | $649 |
| Managed server, each | $149 |

### Cited Findings
- **Micro-IT** (Paducah KY area; the page's address data is inconsistent; page updated 2026-11-24) publishes a full price list. Fetched. — [Micro-IT IT cost article](https://micro-it.net/resources/article-it-cost)
  - **Managed Site**, covering firewall, switching and wireless:
    - Small: **$149/location/month** (1 on-site hour).
    - Standard: **$329** (3 on-site hours).
    - Complex: **$649** (6 on-site hours).
    - 5% off each additional site.
    - **$895** one-time minimum setup per site.
  - **Managed Server: $149/server/month.**
  - Backup appliance: $279 per 2 TB.
  - Managed Endpoint: $79/device.
  - Managed Inbox: $20/mailbox.
  - Network devices and VMs have no separate price; they are covered by the site fee.
  - Minimum for healthcare clients: $750/month.
  - Term discounts: 5% for 24 months, 10% for 36 months, 3% for annual prepay.
- **Onsite Technical Services** (Phoenix; Oct 2026) prices network management as a **separate per-site line**. Fetched. — [OTS](https://www.onsite-tech.com/2026/10/18/network-management-separate-line-item-managed-it/)
  - Client-owned firewall, switches and access points: **about $50-$200/site/month**.
  - OTS-supplied hardware: **about $150-$400/site/month**.
  - Rationale: "network infrastructure scales with locations and complexity, not user count". A separate line stops simple single-site clients from subsidising complex multi-site ones.
- **EasyITGuys** says some providers bundle office network support, including firewall management and on-site support, into one per-location fee. On-site support can cost 10-20% more than remote-only. Fetched. — [EasyITGuys](https://easyitguys.com/pricing/part-3-it-pricing-deep-dive/)
- **Acronis** describes monitoring-only as one of the cheapest MSP models: the provider monitors and alerts, and the client's own IT team remediates. Snippet. — [Acronis MSP pricing models](https://www.acronis.com/en-us/articles/msp-pricing-models/)

### Inferences
These are suggestions, not sourced facts:
- A model that maps onto Akimbo's three tiers:
  - **Essentials**: a per-site network fee, for monitoring and alerting, firmware, and config backup.
  - **Managed**: a higher per-site fee for full change management, security policy and some on-site time.
  - **Managed + Servers**: the site fee plus a per-server or per-host fee, with VMs either included or charged a small per-VM fee.
- Per-user pricing fits poorly. A 60-person office with one firewall and four switches costs little more to manage than a 15-person one, and the local per-user benchmarks ($100-$300) include help desk, which Akimbo does not offer.
- Illustrative single-site figures, built from the cited figures:
  - **Essentials**: about $150-$250/month. Anchors: Micro-IT Small $149; OTS $50-$200 for client-owned gear; EasyITGuys $125-$250 per flat network.
  - **Managed**: about $300-$650/month. Anchors: Micro-IT Standard $329 and Complex $649; OTS up to $400.
  - **Servers**: about $150-$250 per physical server or host per month on top. Anchors: Micro-IT $149; Kaseya average $156 (old); WaTech $200; ARPHost $100-$400.
- A line-item cross-check gives about **$345/month** for one firewall, three switches and six access points: $150 + 3×$45 + 6×$10, using the unverified SpectrumVoIP figures. That fits inside the per-site ranges above.

### Gaps
- I found no infrastructure-only MSP in North Carolina with published prices. All per-site comparables are out of region: Kentucky/Illinois and Arizona.

## 4. What would a 10-100 staff business typically pay per month in total?

### Takeaway
- **Full managed IT** for 10-100 staff typically totals **about $1,000-$5,000+/month at the small end** (roughly $2,000-$6,000 for 20-30 people) and scales to five figures near 100 users, at $100-$250/user.
- **Infrastructure-only service** for a single-site business in this band would, by the comparables, total about **$150-$650/month for network**, plus **about $150-$250 per server**. That is roughly **$300-$1,200/month** for a typical one-site, one-to-three-server SMB.

### Cited Findings
- Triad full managed IT totals. Fetched, except Petronella (snippet). — [Trinity](https://trinitysolutionsinc.com/managed-it-services-in-winston-salem/); [Petronella Greensboro](https://petronellatech.com/blog/it-services-greensboro-nc-complete-guide-managed-it-support-triad-businesses/)
  - **20 people: $2,000-$5,000/month** (Trinity, High Point).
  - **30 people: $3,000-$6,000/month** (Petronella).
- Winston-Salem directory estimate: **$1,000-$5,000/month** for small businesses with 10-50 employees. Snippet. — [bestitmsps.com](https://www.bestitmsps.com/us/managed-it/winston-salem/)
- Micro-IT published totals for remote teams (endpoint and mailbox based, not infrastructure): **10 people $1,019; 25 people $2,504; 50 people $4,979** per month. Fetched. — [Micro-IT](https://micro-it.net/resources/article-it-cost)
- OTS 20-user example in Phoenix, full scope including network: **about $3,050-$4,200/month** with client-owned hardware, or **$3,150-$4,400** with OTS hardware. The network line is only $50-$400 of that. Fetched. — [OTS](https://www.onsite-tech.com/2026/10/18/network-management-separate-line-item-managed-it/)
- Sequentur estimates the network layer alone at **$400-$1,500/month** for a single site with 10-50 users. Snippet. — [Sequentur](https://www.sequentur.com/managed-network-services-for-small-business)

### Inferences
- In the OTS example, network management is about **2-10% of a full managed IT bill**. That means an infrastructure-only MSP's price is a small fraction of the local per-user totals buyers will compare it against.
- That makes a published low "from" price look attractive. It also risks buyers anchoring on it as a substitute for full managed IT, which it is not.
- Multi-site clients and clients with 3+ servers or hypervisor clusters would push totals toward $1,000-$2,000+/month.

### Gaps
- I found no source that reports actual spend by 10-100 staff firms on network or server-only management, as opposed to full-stack IT.

## 5. Does publishing "starting at" prices help or hurt small MSPs?

### Takeaway
Industry commentary is split and almost entirely opinion, with no controlled data:
- MSP marketing voices argue against publishing because it loses leads and strips out context.
- Others argue for it because buyers dislike "contact us" and it filters out poor-fit prospects.
- Even the "against" side concedes that a **minimum or "from" price** helps screen out tire-kickers.
- Standardised, tiered offers are the easiest to publish.

### Cited Findings
- **Against**: Nate Freedman of MSP Sites (SmarterMSP, Aug 18, 2020; older) says a website's job is to "generate as many leads as possible". Prices remove a reason to call, case studies sell better, and low listed prices can signal low quality. His only evidence is anecdotal; he says he has worked with 100+ MSPs. Fetched. — [SmarterMSP](https://smartermsp.com/keep-your-prices-off-your-msp-website/)
- **Mostly against, with a "minimum price" exception**: Technibble (Sept 2022; older). Fetched. — [Technibble](https://www.technibble.com/advertise-msp-pricing-online/)
  - Posted prices lack context and can look too high for "just some monitoring, backup, and edge protection".
  - Prices that look too low can signal an inexperienced or tiny provider.
  - **But** listing a minimum, e.g. "a minimum of $150 per seat and upwards", can screen out tire-kickers.
  - No data is cited.
- **For**: JumpCloud's blog says "contact for pricing" turns buyers off, and publishing helps an MSP stand out because most hide prices. Snippet. — [JumpCloud MSP pricing](https://jumpcloud.com/blog/msp-pricing)
- **General services commentary**:
  - HubSpot argues for publishing pricing. — [HubSpot](https://blog.hubspot.com/marketing/why-publish-pricing-on-website-var)
  - Liston Witherill says publish only if you have more leads than you need and the service is well defined and contained. — [Medium, Witherill](https://medium.com/@listonw/when-you-should-and-shouldnt-publish-prices-on-your-website-6cac11c917d4)

  Snippet.
- Locally, no Triad MSP I found publishes firm prices (see section 1). Petronella uses "Starting at a per-seat monthly rate" with no number, which is a middle path. — [Petronella plans](https://petronellatech.com/it-services/managed-it-services-plans/)

### Inferences
- Akimbo's offer is standardised and device or site-scoped, with three defined tiers. That is the case where commentators say publishing is easiest to justify.
- A **"from $X/mo per site"** price with "final quote after audit" combines the screening benefit with a route to a conversation.
- Akimbo's numbers are far below local per-user totals, so a published price risks looking "too cheap" (the Technibble and Freedman concern). It needs framing around scope: no help desk, infrastructure only.
- There would be first-mover visibility in a market where no Triad firm publishes prices.

### Gaps
- I found no quantitative evidence, such as conversion rates or A/B tests, on published versus hidden pricing for MSPs.
- The r/msp threads did not surface in search.
- The anti-publishing sources are 2020-2022, so flag them as older.
