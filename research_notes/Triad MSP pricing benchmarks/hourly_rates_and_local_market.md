# Triad (Winston-Salem / Greensboro NC) hourly and project rates for IT/network work, and the local MSP landscape

Researched 2026-11-24. Unless labeled, figures are Triad-specific. Most rate data comes from vendor pages and cost-guide aggregators, not surveys. Treat it as anchor-quality, not market statistics.

## 1. Hourly rates: on-site tech, network engineer, remote, after-hours, trip fees (Triad vs national vs Charlotte/Raleigh)

### Takeaway
Only one Triad business-facing firm publishes hourly rates: Piedmont Technical Experts (Greensboro) charges $125/hr remote and $150/hr on-site, and labels these residential/standard rates. A Winston-Salem network firm lists $150–$199/hr on Clutch. National MSP guides put project/network engineers at $125–$250/hr, scheduled on-site work at $150–$250/hr with minimums, and after-hours at 1.25x–2x. A defensible Triad anchor is about $125–$150/hr for remote or on-site tech work and $150–$195/hr for network-engineer or project work, with after-hours at 1.5x and emergencies/holidays at 2x. I found no published Charlotte/Raleigh billing rates; the BLS wage data in section 3 suggests Charlotte and Raleigh labor costs are similar to or slightly above the Triad's.

### Cited Findings

**National (not Triad)**
- Project engineer rate: $125–$250/hr, with the low end for routine deployments and the high end for architecture, cloud migration or security work. — [Managed Services Pricing Guide 2025](https://managedservicesnearyou.com/pricing-guide) (no sources cited; unverified)
- Same guide, on-site and after-hours terms:
  - Scheduled on-site: $150–$250/hr, 2-hour minimum, plus travel outside the service area.
  - Emergency on-site: $250–$400/hr, 4-hour minimum.
  - After-hours: evenings (6–10pm) at 1.25x–1.5x, overnight/weekend at 1.5x–2x, holidays at 2x–2.5x.

### Inferences
- The Triad market has a visible $125 (remote) / $150 (on-site) anchor, set by a firm whose published prices are residential. Business rates from established MSPs are probably the same or higher, roughly $150–$195. Pricing at $125–$150 for tech work and $165–$195 for senior network engineering would sit mid-market.
- "No trip charge" is a stated local competitive claim (Trinity). A visible dispatch fee may put Akimbo at a disadvantage inside Forsyth/Guilford. Alternatives are a 1-hour on-site minimum instead of a trip fee, or mileage charged only outside the Triad (Piedmont's model).
- A common local and national convention is after-hours at 1.5x and emergencies/holidays at 2x, with a 1–2 hour minimum on-site and a larger minimum for emergency call-outs.

### Gaps
- I found no Triad firm publishing a business (not residential) hourly rate, an after-hours rate or an explicit trip-fee dollar amount.
- I found no Charlotte- or Raleigh-specific published billing rates. The comparison rests only on the BLS wage data in section 3.
- I did not retrieve r/msp or r/sysadmin rate threads; the search surfaced none. The Kaseya benchmark figure is second-hand.

## 2. Project pricing: firewall, switch/Wi-Fi refresh, cabling per drop, server migration/virtualisation, block-hour discounts

### Takeaway
I found no Triad-specific project pricing. National and other-region benchmarks:
- Commercial Cat6 drop: about $150–$300 installed (IBISWorld 2026 benchmark $197/drop; Greer SC $125–$250).
- Wi-Fi AP installation labor: about $100–$500 per AP. Installed per-AP totals run about $250–$1,000 for typical interior SMB installs, plus the drop if new cabling is needed.
- Firewall hardware for 15–100 users: $1,500–$4,000. Total firewall/router upgrade projects run $3,000–$15,000.
- SMB network infrastructure refresh: $5,000–$20,000.
- Server-to-cloud migration: $5,000–$25,000+ per server.
- Block-hour prepay: 10–20% off standard rate, in 20/40/80-hour blocks.

### Cited Findings
**Structured cabling (national/other-region)**
- IBISWorld's 2026 benchmark market price for network cable installation is **$197 per drop**. — [IBISWorld](https://www.ibisworld.com/united-states/procurement/network-cable-installation/48688842/)
- Commercial Cat6 drops run $150–$300 installed (cable, termination, testing, labor), and Cat6A runs about $200–$400 per drop. Per-drop cost falls on larger jobs. — [Unio Digital cabling benchmark](https://unio.digital/blog/structured-cabling-cost-benchmark); [Unio per-drop](https://unio.digital/blog/structured-cabling-cost-per-drop)
- Another planning range is $150–$250 per grouped drop. — [DataWire Solutions 2026](https://datawiresolutions.com/blog/network-cabling-cost)
- Nearest regional data point: Greer, SC (Upstate SC) Cat6 is $125–$250 per drop, fully installed and tested. — [Swivl Greer SC](https://swivl.tech/cost-guide/structured-cabling/greer-sc)
- Instrata, an NC cabling contractor, publishes no dollar figures and quotes only after consultation. — [Instrata NC pricing](https://www.instrata.com/instrata-pricing-what-to-expect-in-north-carolina/)

**Wi-Fi / access points (national)**
- Labor is $100–$250 per AP, and most businesses land at $250–$500 per AP for hardware plus labor. Small offices under 5,000 sq ft land at $2,500–$6,000 total (APs, basic cabling, switch). This is a Chicago guide. — [UniFi Nerds Chicago](https://unifinerds.com/unifi-installation-cost-chicago/)
- Professional installation is $200–$500 per AP, including configuration and testing. — [The Network Installers](https://thenetworkinstallers.com/blog/how-much-does-wifi-installation-cost/)
- Fully installed commercial Wi-Fi is $650–$3,500 per AP, including hardware, cabling and a share of switching. The cable drop alone is $150–$400 per AP. — [Netfor business WiFi guide](https://www.netfor.com/resource-center/blog/business-wifi-installation/)
- Business AP hardware is about $150–$500 per unit. — (same search set; vendor-blog quality)

**Firewall (national)**
- Fortinet: a small-business appliance is about $700–$1,000, and a business with 15–100 users may pay $1,500–$4,000 for hardware. — [Fortinet firewall pricing](https://www.fortinet.com/uk/products/network-firewall-pricing) (via search summary)
- A small/midsize firewall or router upgrade project, including hardware and labor to configure, install and test, is estimated at $3,000–$15,000. — (search summary of [BD Emerson](https://www.bdemerson.com/article/how-much-does-a-firewall-cost) / [Huntress](https://huntress.com/cybersecurity-101/topic/firewall-costs); exact attribution among these not verified)

**Server, migration and refresh projects (national)**
- [Managed Services Pricing Guide 2025](https://managedservicesnearyou.com/pricing-guide) (unsourced):
  - Server-to-cloud migration: $5,000–$25,000+ per server.
  - SMB network infrastructure refresh (switches, APs, firewall, cabling): $5,000–$20,000.
  - New office buildout: $10,000–$50,000+.
  - M365/Workspace migration: $2,000–$10,000.
- The same guide gives no on-prem virtualisation (Hyper-V/VMware/Proxmox host build) price.

**Block-hour prepay (national)**
- Prepaid blocks are typically 10–20% below the standard hourly rate. Common blocks are 20, 40 or 80 hours, with unused hours rolling over for at least 90 days. — [Managed Services Pricing Guide 2025](https://managedservicesnearyou.com/pricing-guide)

### Inferences
- These are anchors for quoting labor, not hardware:
  - Per AP: $150–$250 installed and configured, excluding hardware and the drop.
  - Per drop: $175–$250 for Cat6, handled by Akimbo or subcontracted.
  - Firewall swap: about 6–12 engineer hours, so roughly $1,000–$2,500 labor at $165–$195/hr, plus hardware and licensing.
  - These anchors are in line with the national ranges above.
- At an engineer rate of $165–$195/hr, a 10%/15% discount on 20/40-hour blocks would land near $150–$165/hr effective.

### Gaps
- I found no Triad- or NC-specific per-drop, per-AP, firewall or server-migration price published by a local firm.
- I found no source for on-prem server virtualisation project pricing, for example a single-host Hyper-V/Proxmox build with VM migration.
- Fixr/HomeGuide/Angi NC pages for cabling or Wi-Fi were not retrieved. Fixr has a national Cat6 page: [Fixr Cat6](https://www.fixr.com/costs/upgrading-to-cat6-cabling).

## 3. Local labor-cost context: BLS OEWS wages, Winston-Salem MSA and Greensboro-High Point MSA vs US (May 2025)

### Takeaway
Triad IT wages run about 3–10% below the US average:
- Network/systems administrators: mean $48.42/hr in Winston-Salem and $44.87/hr in Greensboro-High Point, vs $49.85 US.
- Computer user support specialists: mean $29.34 and $28.02/hr, vs $32.37 US.
- Network support specialists: mean $36.24 and $35.55/hr, vs $39.36 US.

A $150/hr billing rate is about 3.1–3.3x a Triad sysadmin's mean wage and about 5x a help-desk tech's.

### Inferences
- A rough sanity check uses fully loaded cost of about 1.3–1.4x wage, so a Triad sysadmin costs about $60–$68/hr. At 60–70% billable utilisation, the break-even bill rate is about $90–$110/hr. A $150–$195 engineer rate therefore gives healthy margin, and $125/hr for tech-level work covers a user-support-level employee (about $38–$41/hr loaded) comfortably.
- Raleigh sysadmin wages run about 6–15% above the Triad, and Charlotte's are similar to or slightly below. Charlotte/Raleigh MSPs plausibly bill similar to or modestly above Triad rates, but I found no direct billing-rate evidence.

### Gaps
- No Charlotte/Raleigh employment counts were retrieved, and no US employment count for 15-1241.
- The BLS bulk XLSX download was blocked by bot protection. The values came from the API, are May 2025 and are not independently cross-checked against the HTML tables.

## 4. Who serves Triad small businesses, and how big is the 10–100 employee market?

### Takeaway
- The Triad MSP field is crowded with generalist "managed IT + cybersecurity" firms: Dynamic Quest (largest, Greensboro HQ, multi-state), Beacon IT Services, Trinity Solutions, First Column IT, Total Computer Solutions, Mindcore, a CMIT Solutions franchise, and Petronella Tech and Netfriends targeting the area from outside.
- None found presents as infrastructure-only, and almost none publish business prices.
- CBP 2023 counts about **6,600 establishments with 10–99 employees in Forsyth + Guilford** (2,575 + 4,025), and about 9,900 across the nine-county Triad.

### Inferences
- I found no Triad MSP that positions itself as infrastructure/network-only. The visible competitors are full-stack (help desk + M365 + security + compliance) MSPs, and several are large or out-of-region (Dynamic Quest multi-state; Petronella and Netfriends from the Triangle). That leaves a positioning gap for a founder-led network/server specialist. A co-managed or "subcontract to generalist MSPs" angle may also be viable, since Beacon already markets co-managed IT.
- CBP counts establishments (locations), not firms. Some 10–99 employee sites belong to larger companies with internal IT. The addressable count of independent 10–100 staff firms is therefore below 6,600, plausibly a few thousand in Forsyth/Guilford. Many sectors with 10–99 staff (restaurants, retail) also have minimal network needs.

### Gaps
- I could not retrieve the current Triad Business Journal "largest IT/computer services firms" list (paywalled or not indexed), so I have no current local rankings, headcounts or revenue figures.
- Employee counts for most named firms were not found.
