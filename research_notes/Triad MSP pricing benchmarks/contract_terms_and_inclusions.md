# Small-business MSP contract terms and service-level inclusions (Piedmont Triad focus, plus national norms)

Researched 2026-11-24. Most sources are MSPs' own marketing pages, MSP vendor blogs (Kaseya, N-able, SmarterMSP, Giant Rocketship) or law-firm and template commentary. Very few Triad MSPs publish real contract terms; nearly all say "contact us". I found no r/msp threads through search. I found no CompTIA or ChannelE2E survey that gives the split of IT MSP contract lengths. "TRIAD" marks local data. "NC" marks other North Carolina data. "NATIONAL" marks US and other data.

## 1. Contract length, auto-renewal, notice periods and early termination fees

### Takeaway
The national default is a 1-3 year term that auto-renews, with 30-60 days' notice. Early exit usually costs a percentage of the fees left in the term (50% is a common figure). In the Triad, Trinity Solutions (High Point) is the only MSP I found that clearly and repeatedly advertises month-to-month terms. It explicitly attacks local "2-3 year" lock-ins, so month-to-month is a live differentiator here but not one Akimbo would have alone. No Triad MSP I found publishes a notice period.

### Cited Findings
**TRIAD**
- Trinity Solutions (High Point, 1224 Eastchester Dr; serves Greensboro, Winston-Salem, Kernersville, Asheboro) says: "Every client can leave at the end of any month." "Most High Point IT companies lock you into 2–3 year agreements." Its FAQ says "month-to-month contracts with no long-term lock-in." Consulting is "Month-to-month only." The page states no cancellation notice period. — [Trinity Solutions High Point page](https://trinitysolutionsinc.com/managed-it-services-in-high-point/)
- Trinity's Clutch profile says "Month-to-month contracts. No multi-year lock-in." and "We don't lock clients into multi-year contracts." — [Clutch: Trinity Solutions](https://clutch.co/profile/trinity-solutions)
- Trinity cites $100-300/user/month typical pricing and $2,000-5,000/month for a 20-person office (pricing noted only in passing). — [Trinity Solutions High Point page](https://trinitysolutionsinc.com/managed-it-services-in-high-point/)
- manageditservicesgreensboro.com (a Greensboro-branded site whose operator is unclear) says its plans have "no contracts" but charge $200-500 setup fees. Per the search snippet, plans run $99-499/month. I did not fetch the full page. — [manageditservicesgreensboro.com](https://www.manageditservicesgreensboro.com/it-support-for-small-businesses/)
- A CloudSecureTech directory page for Greensboro says contract lengths among local MSPs range from month-to-month to three years (search snippet only). — [CloudSecureTech Greensboro directory](https://www.cloudsecuretech.com/us/it-services/managed/greensboro/)
- IDeACOM NC (Winston-Salem page) and Petronella Technology Group (Raleigh HQ at 5540 Centerview Dr, with a Winston-Salem landing page) state no contract length or notice period. Petronella's footer says "All Sales are final", which seems to refer to custom orders. — [IDeACOM Winston-Salem](https://ideacomnc.com/managed-it-services/winston-salem/); [Petronella Winston-Salem](https://petronellatech.com/managed-it-services-winston-salem-nc/)

**NC (outside the Triad)**
- Net Friends (Durham; has a Winston-Salem location page) says: "You won't find any contracts to lock you into sub-par performance" and that it will "earn your business every month." Added services are rolled into "a single contract with one invoice and one term." It also charges an onboarding fee of "about 1 month's worth of services", with more if onboarding runs past about 4 weeks. — [Net Friends NetVisor pricing](https://www.netfriends.com/pricing-netvisor-managed-it)

**NATIONAL**
- Notice: "most providers ask for a 30-day notice period", with 60 days used in some longer contracts. The author criticises MSPs who quietly let the notice window pass to force a renewal. A 1-year term "is not unusual", and some MSPs ask for 3 years. (Richard Tubb, April 2020.) — [SmarterMSP](https://smartermsp.com/how-long-should-my-msp-contracts-last/)
- Law-firm template guidance: termination for convenience needs notice "usually 60 days" plus an early termination fee, "typically a percentage of the fees remaining", with 50% recommended. No fee applies when the customer terminates for cause, such as persistent SLA failures. The firm recommends 3-year terms, with a 20% premium for 1-year and 25% for month-to-month. Annual price escalators have traditionally run 3-8%, and its template allows up to 10%. Auto-renewal is typically month-to-month or another one-year term. (Scott & Scott LLP, 2022.) — [Scott & Scott LLP](https://scottandscottllp.com/termination-clauses-in-it-managed-services-contracts/)
- MSP blog (Intelligent Technical Solutions, February 2026): "many MSPs lock clients into one-, three-, or even five-year contracts"; "Some contracts automatically renew for another full term unless you provide notice months in advance". It reports early termination fees of "50% (or more) of the remaining contract value". ITS itself offers 1-, 2- and 3-year terms with auto-renew and break clauses. — [ITS blog](https://www.itsasap.com/blog/it-contracts-exit-terms-explained)
- Kaseya (June 2026) says: "Month-to-month contracts, while flexible, often come with higher costs and increased risk." It also says vendors reward annual or multi-year commitments. It gives no statistics. — [Kaseya blog](https://www.kaseya.com/blog/why-msps-should-revisit-contract-terms-before-going-month-to-month/)
- Search summaries of other commentary say "Thirty days is common. Sixty and ninety are creeping in. Some now want 180." (Source page not fetched; treat as indicative.) — [makologics](https://makologics.com/blog/why-msp-contracts-have-minimum-terms-and-when-they-shouldnt)

### Inferences
- Publishing "month-to-month, 30 days' notice, no early termination fee" puts Akimbo level with Trinity's local claim and ahead of most Triad competitors, who don't publish terms. Publishing a specific notice period would go further than Trinity, which states none.
- The industry case against month-to-month is the MSP's own risk: onboarding cost and tooling commitments. Net Friends shows the usual answer is a paid onboarding fee instead of lock-in. Akimbo could do the same, for example by paying for onboarding through a separate audit or onboarding fee.
- A 30-day notice period is the national baseline, so offering it costs Akimbo little. Not auto-renewing into a new fixed term is what makes the "no hostage-taking" line credible.

### Gaps
- No survey data on how IT MSP contracts split between month-to-month, 12, 24 and 36 months. Kaseya's 2026 State of the MSP excerpts didn't include it.
- No Triad MSP publishes an MSA or terms page with a notice period or early termination fee.
- r/msp threads weren't reachable through search.

## 2. Support hours, after-hours rates and response-time SLAs

### Takeaway
Small-business plans in the Triad and nationally usually staff the help desk during business hours (roughly 7-7 or 8-5, Monday to Friday) and add 24/7 automated monitoring. Live after-hours help is either emergency-only or limited to higher tiers. Published response targets cluster at 15 minutes to 1 hour for critical issues and 1-4 hours for high priority, with next business day or longer for routine work. Resolution times are rarely guaranteed. Ad hoc after-hours work is billed at about 1.25-2x the normal rate.

### Cited Findings
**TRIAD**
- Trinity Solutions: "Help desk operates during business hours", plus a 24/7 SOC for security alerts. "Standard response targets are 15 minutes for critical issues", 1 hour for high priority and 4 business hours for routine requests (self-reported). — [Clutch: Trinity Solutions](https://clutch.co/profile/trinity-solutions)
- IT Solutions (a national MSP with a Winston-Salem landing page) publishes a Service-Level Objectives document. The help desk is staffed "on weekdays between 7:00 AM and 7:00 PM Eastern". Monitoring runs 24/7/365 and sends critical alerts to on-call engineers within 15 minutes. "Automated alerts received after 11 PM are responded to ... at 7:00 AM." An after-hours emergency number reaches an on-call engineer and manager 24/7/365. The document says it "cannot provide a guaranteed resolution time" for critical events. (2023 document.) — [IT Solutions SLO PDF](http://www.itsolutions-inc.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/07/IT-Solutions-%E2%80%93-Service-Level-Objectives-%E2%80%93-Managed-IT-Services.pdf); [IT Solutions Winston-Salem page](https://www.itsolutions-inc.com/welcome/winston-salem-nc)
- IDeACOM NC claims "Clear SLAs" but publishes no figures. Petronella advertises "24/7 monitoring" but gives no response figures. ITSco offers "customized SLAs". Per search snippets, Cyber Solutions advertises a response SLA of "less than an hour". — [IDeACOM](https://ideacomnc.com/managed-it-services/winston-salem/); [Petronella](https://petronellatech.com/managed-it-services-winston-salem-nc/); [ITSco](https://www.itsco.com/managed-it-services-winston-salem); [Cyber Solutions](https://discovercybersolutions.com/north-carolina/it-company-in-winston-salem-nc)

**NC**
- Net Friends: "24x7 coverage for one predictable monthly rate". "Nights, weekends, and holidays cost you nothing extra." "Anything critical gets a guaranteed response within 30 minutes." — [Net Friends](https://www.netfriends.com/pricing-netvisor-managed-it)
- NC state IT SLA templates (government, for reference only): a 15-minute acknowledgement and 4-hour resolution target for critical M365 incidents, and a 4-hour on-site response, 24/7, in a hardware maintenance contract (per search summary). — [it.nc.gov](https://it.nc.gov/media/3102/open)

**NATIONAL**
- ITsupportRI (Rhode Island) publishes a business-hours SLA covering Monday-Friday 8:00 AM to 5:00 PM, with weekends closed. First response is "15 Minutes or Less" at every priority. Resolution targets are 8 hours (Urgent), 12 hours (High), 24 hours (Medium) and 48 hours (Low). — [ITsupportRI SLA](https://www.itsupportri.com/sla)
- Better World Technology (Oak Brook, IL) SLA table: P1 1 hour response / 24 hours resolution ("24/7 response"); P2 2 hours / 36 hours; P3 6 hours / 5 business days; P4 8 hours / 7 business days. Its FAQ contradicts this with 15 minutes, 1 hour and 4 business hours. After-hours emergency support is "available 24/7 for clients on applicable plans". — [Better World Technology SLA](https://www.betterworldtechnology.com/sla)
- N-able tier estimates: "Standard" plans give business-hours response; "Advanced" plans add after-hours support; "Premium" plans add a 24/7 SOC. N-able also notes that a small business may find a full SLA more trouble than it's worth. — [N-able MSP pricing guide](https://www.n-able.com/blog/msp-pricing-guide); [N-able SLA guide](https://www.n-able.com/blog/smart-msps-guide-service-level-agreements)
- After-hours multipliers: NetFire bills scheduled after-hours work at 1.5x and unscheduled or emergency work at 2x, plus $1.49/mile travel to all on-site visits. Easy IT Guys bills planned after-hours work at 1.25x and emergencies at 2x. Examples of after-hours on-site rates: $225/hour with a 1-hour minimum, and $250/hour with a 2-hour minimum. — [NetFire fee schedule](https://netfire.com/support-center/guides/tech-market/managed-technology/fee-schedule); [Easy IT Guys](https://easyitguys.com/docs/hourly-support-rates/); [Matrix Integration rates](https://www.matrixintegration.com/mspservice/)
- Inteligentic (Miami; small example, treat with caution): Starter plan has business-hours remote support and an 8-hour SLA; Business plan has a 4-hour SLA and on-site; Enterprise plan has a 24/7 help desk, a 2-hour SLA and 2 on-site visits/month. — [Inteligentic MSP plans](https://inteligentic-web.pages.dev/pages/msp)

### Inferences
- A realistic, defensible commitment for a solo, founder-led MSP is business-hours coverage, for example Monday-Friday 8-5 or 8-6, plus 24/7 monitoring that alerts the founder. Critical outages could get a best-effort after-hours response. The 15-30 minute critical-response claims (Trinity, Net Friends) come from multi-staff shops. A sole operator who publishes them risks failing to meet them.
- Infrastructure-only scope (network, servers) suits severity tiers based on outage scope: whole site down vs. degraded vs. a single request. Response-only targets with no resolution guarantee are standard and defensible (IT Solutions states this explicitly).
- A natural tier lever: Essentials gets business hours only and bills after-hours at 1.5x; Managed + Servers includes emergency after-hours response for critical outages.

### Gaps
- No Triad MSP publishes hourly or after-hours rates.
- I didn't verify whether Trinity's 15-minute target means acknowledgement or engagement by an engineer.

## 3. On-site visits: allowances, trip charges, service radius

### Takeaway
Two models dominate. One is "unlimited on-site within a radius" for all-inclusive plans: Net Friends (50 miles), Petronella and Trinity (no trip charges). The other is on-site visits reserved for higher tiers, as a fixed count or block of hours, with per-visit or mileage charges below that tier. Local competitors explicitly advertise "no trip charges" and same-day on-site, so charging trip fees on the top tier would look poor locally.

### Cited Findings
**TRIAD**
- Trinity Solutions: "No trip charges. No fine print." Return visits for the same issue within 5 days are free. "We can be at your High Point office same day when the situation calls for it." Most work is done remotely. — [Trinity High Point](https://trinitysolutionsinc.com/managed-it-services-in-high-point/)
- IDeACOM NC: on-site work is reserved for the top tier ("Complete: Secure + on-site hours, vCIO, quarterly reviews"). The page also claims "Unlimited remote help desk with on-site support in Winston-Salem & Forsyth County". — [IDeACOM](https://ideacomnc.com/managed-it-services/winston-salem/)
- Petronella: "Unlimited remote and on-site support with no per-ticket fees". "Routine on-site visits are included", with urgent on-site visits handled "on a priority basis". This comes from a Raleigh-based firm. — [Petronella](https://petronellatech.com/managed-it-services-winston-salem-nc/)

**NC**
- Net Friends: on-site is "included at no additional charge for locations within 50 miles of our headquarters in Durham", and beyond that a "per-visit travel surcharge" applies (amount not stated). — [Net Friends](https://www.netfriends.com/pricing-netvisor-managed-it)

**NATIONAL**
- NetFire charges $1.49/mile on all on-site visits from its nearest dispatch location. — [NetFire](https://netfire.com/support-center/guides/tech-market/managed-technology/fee-schedule)
- PNW Computers includes on-site visits within 15 miles at no extra charge. — [PNW Computers ToS](https://pnw-computers.github.io/IT-Manual/policies/Terms-of-Service/)
- Tier patterns: a "Silver" middle tier adds on-site visits and a "Gold" tier adds 24/7 emergency support. Inteligentic includes 2 on-site visits/month in its top tier and bills extra visits. A GitHub-hosted small-shop page charges $50/visit on Basic and offers unlimited visits on Premium (low reliability). — [Huntress MSP pricing guide](https://www.huntress.com/msp-guide/building-your-msp-pricing-model); [Inteligentic](https://inteligentic-web.pages.dev/pages/msp); [HC IT Pros](https://elspaniard97.github.io/hc-it-pros/pricing.html)

### Inferences
- For Akimbo, a radius-based promise fits the founder's real travel capacity, for example "on-site included within 30-40 miles of Winston-Salem/Greensboro; mileage beyond". The Triad core (Winston-Salem to Greensboro is about 30 miles, High Point and Kernersville in between) fits inside a modest radius.
- One tier ladder that matches local norms: Essentials gets remote support, with on-site billed hourly and no trip fee inside the radius. Managed gets on-site for covered network incidents. Managed + Servers gets on-site for covered server and network incidents, plus scheduled visits such as a quarterly review on site. "Unlimited" claims are common locally, but a solo operator should cap them by scope ("for covered infrastructure incidents") rather than by count.

### Gaps
- No Triad MSP publishes a trip-charge amount or an exact radius.
- No source gives a typical count of included visits or hours by tier beyond single examples.

## 4. Offboarding and exit terms (documentation, credentials, fees)

### Takeaway
The main customer complaint about leaving an MSP is withheld documentation or passwords, and slow or paid data handover. MSP-side guidance says to include basic handover (passwords plus environment documentation) in the recurring fee and to bill hourly for extras. I found no Triad MSP that publishes an offboarding commitment. This is the clearest gap Akimbo can fill to back up its "no hostage-taking" message.

### Cited Findings
- **NATIONAL** (MSP guidance, Giant Rocketship, March 2025): handover of passwords and environment documentation should be in the recurring fee. Training the incoming MSP should not. "Any extra assistance beyond the basic handoff will be billed separately". Handover meetings should be capped at 1-2 hours. Internal SOPs, scripts and proprietary configuration can be withheld. RMM and agents should be removed on the handoff day. Backup data is deleted after a set period, with extended retention prepaid. Offboarding waits until balances are paid. — [Giant Rocketship](https://giantrocketship.com/blog/defend-yourself-from-abusive-msp-client-offboardings)
- **NATIONAL**: former customers "often accuse MSPs of refusing to hand over documentation or changing passwords right before the transition". One N-able author says most would agree an "offboarding fee" shouldn't be in a managed services contract. A flat transition fee is suggested as an alternative to hourly billing. — [N-able: How to offboard a customer](https://www.n-able.com/blog/how-offboard-customer); [SuperOps](https://superops.com/blog/tips-for-good-msp-offboarding-process)
- **NATIONAL** (ITS, February 2026): some providers charge "excessive data transfer fees or delay the handover". Customers are advised to get written confirmation that "all data, passwords, and documentation belong to you and will be transferred upon request". ITS says it joins handover meetings with the new provider. — [ITS blog](https://www.itsasap.com/blog/it-contracts-exit-terms-explained)
- **TRIAD**: Trinity describes onboarding a client from a previous provider ("gaining access to systems and documenting the environment", 2-4 weeks) but publishes no offboarding terms. Petronella says nothing on handover. — [Trinity High Point](https://trinitysolutionsinc.com/managed-it-services-in-high-point/); [Petronella](https://petronellatech.com/managed-it-services-winston-salem-nc/)
- **NC**: Net Friends lists device, user and license offboarding (employee offboarding) as included, but gives no client-exit terms. — [Net Friends](https://www.netfriends.com/pricing-netvisor-managed-it)
- **NATIONAL** (law firm): a no-fee exit for cause, such as persistent SLA misses, is standard even in long contracts. — [Scott & Scott LLP](https://scottandscottllp.com/termination-clauses-in-it-managed-services-contracts/)

### Inferences
- A published exit clause would set Akimbo apart in the Triad. Possible terms: documentation and all admin credentials handed over within X business days of notice, at no charge; up to 1-2 hours of handover call included; further help at the standard hourly rate; Akimbo's agents removed on the exit date; backups retained for N days. This matches MSP best practice and uses the language customers are told to demand.
- "Documentation the customer keeps" is stronger if it's live during the contract (for example, a client-accessible documentation export), not just handed over at exit.

### Gaps
- No source gives typical offboarding fee amounts or transition hourly rates.
- No Triad MSP terms page addresses data return or handover timing.

## 5. How small-business MSP tier tables are structured

### Takeaway
Tiers are usually cumulative ("X + ..."), three at most. They move from monitoring and patching, to security and backup, to strategic or vCIO work, on-site time and after-hours coverage. Support hours, response-time targets and on-site inclusion are the levers most often used to tell tiers apart. Some competitors deliberately avoid tiers (Net Friends: "no add-on tiers").

### Cited Findings
- **TRIAD** IDeACOM NC: Core ("Monitoring, patching, help desk, M365 admin"), then Secure ("Core + EDR, email security, backup & restore testing"), then Complete ("Secure + on-site hours, vCIO, quarterly reviews"). Priced per user or device and quoted after a consult, with a 25% non-profit discount. — [IDeACOM](https://ideacomnc.com/managed-it-services/winston-salem/)
- **TRIAD** Trinity and Petronella publish no tiers; plans are custom and quoted. — [Trinity](https://trinitysolutionsinc.com/managed-it-services-in-high-point/); [Petronella](https://petronellatech.com/managed-it-services-winston-salem-nc/)
- **NC** Net Friends uses a single all-inclusive plan "with no add-on tiers and nothing to upgrade into", priced in per-user bands (for example $129/user for 4-20 users, $70 for 21-50, $60 for 51-100). — [Net Friends](https://www.netfriends.com/pricing-netvisor-managed-it)
- **NATIONAL** Huntress guide: Bronze is remote support and patching, Silver adds on-site visits, Gold adds 24/7 emergency support. It recommends no more than 3-4 tiers. — [Huntress](https://www.huntress.com/msp-guide/building-your-msp-pricing-model)
- **NATIONAL** N-able: Standard is business-hours response, Advanced adds after-hours support, Premium adds a 24/7 SOC. — [N-able](https://www.n-able.com/blog/msp-pricing-guide)
- **NATIONAL** Inteligentic: response SLA tightens by tier (8 hours, then 4 hours, then 2 hours), and on-site and 24/7 coverage appear only in the upper tiers. — [Inteligentic](https://inteligentic-web.pages.dev/pages/msp)
- **NATIONAL** A Technology Marketing Toolkit comparison chart template bills after-hours work at $195/hour on lower tiers and includes it on higher tiers (dated 2021, partly garbled). — [TMT chart PDF](https://technologymarketingtoolkit.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/01/MSP-Service-Plan-Comparison-Chart.pdf)

### Inferences
- Akimbo's scope-based tiers (Essentials, then Managed, then Managed + Servers) differ from the common security-based ladder. Rows a comparison table could carry: scope covered, monitoring (24/7 for all), help desk hours, critical-response target, after-hours (billed vs. included for critical), on-site (billed vs. included within the radius), reviews (annual vs. quarterly), documentation (included in all tiers), contract terms (month-to-month and 30 days for all tiers).
- Keeping contract terms and documentation the same across all tiers, rather than as upsells, supports the "no hostage-taking" message. Hours, response targets and on-site inclusion are the conventional things to vary by tier.

### Gaps
- No Triad MSP publishes a full tier table with response times or on-site allowances per tier, so local comparisons rest on one example (IDeACOM).
