# Publish prices where Triad rivals stay silent

No Piedmont Triad MSP that we found publishes a firm price for a network audit or a managed plan, so whatever Akimbo publishes for issue 14 will stand out. The local "price" for an assessment is **$0**: rivals give it away as a sales scan. Local per-user figures for full managed IT, with help desk included, cluster at **$100–$300/user/month**, or about **$2,000–$6,000/month for a 20–30 person office**. Those numbers do not fit an infrastructure-only offer. The closest out-of-region comparables price network management **per site, at about $150–$650/month, plus about $150–$250 per server**, so a typical one-site, one-to-three-server client would pay roughly **$300–$1,200/month**. National paid audits for a single office with fewer than 25 endpoints run **$1,500–$3,000**. On terms, the national default is a 1–3 year auto-renewing contract with 30–60 days' notice. One High Point rival already sells against that with month-to-month terms and no trip charges, so Akimbo's "no lock-in" message is necessary but not unique. Triad IT wages sit about 3–10% below the US average, and the one published local hourly anchor is **$125 remote / $150 on-site**. The evidence is thin: it is almost all vendor marketing, the local sample is small, and no Triad firm publishes an infrastructure-only plan. Every recommended value at the end of this report is therefore an inference built from anchors, not a measured market average.

## Triad rivals give the audit away, while national paid audits start at $1,500

**Every Triad-serving firm we found that mentions an assessment offers it free.** Trinity Solutions in High Point offers a "free 30-minute IT assessment" ([Clutch: Trinity Solutions](https://clutch.co/profile/trinity-solutions)). Petronella Technology Group is based in Raleigh but runs Greensboro, Winston-Salem and High Point landing pages. It promises "a detailed quote after a free assessment of your Winston-Salem environment" ([Petronella Winston-Salem](https://petronellatech.com/managed-it-services-winston-salem-nc)). Its pricing page lists Cybersecurity Assessment, HIPAA Risk Assessment and penetration testing with no dollar figures; scope is set "in a scoping call and documented in a statement of work" ([Petronella Pricing](https://petronellatech.com/pricing/)). Enitech in Winston-Salem has advertised a complimentary IT security and cost-reduction assessment ([Newswire: Enitech](https://www.newswire.com/news/enitech-offers-free-it-cost-savings-assessment-to-help-businesses-12584728)). A Clutch Greensboro listing offers a "free network assessment" ([Clutch Greensboro](https://clutch.co/it-services/msp/greensboro?page=3)). Charlotte and the Triangle follow the same pattern: Apex Technology offers a network assessment "at no charge and no obligation" ([trustapex.com](https://www.trustapex.com/network-assessment)). That makes a direct NC regional price comparison impossible.

Free and paid assessments are different products. Sequentur, a multi-office MSP with an Asheville office, calls the free version "a scoping conversation plus a high-level scan, not a full engagement". Its paid version delivers a network diagram, a full device inventory with firmware and end-of-life dates, severity-ranked findings, and a 12–24 month roadmap. It publishes the clearest national price ladder: **$1,500–$3,000 for a single office under 25 endpoints, $3,000–$5,000 for 25–75 endpoints, and $5,000–$7,500 for multi-site setups with 75–200 endpoints** ([Sequentur](https://www.sequentur.com/what-is-a-network-assessment-and-why-your-business-should-have-one)). RIT Company, a Chicagoland MSP, puts a typical SMB network security assessment at **$1,500–$6,000**. It notes that an MSP "baseline security audit" is "often free" and that penetration tests run $5,000–$30,000 ([RIT Company](https://ritcompany.com/blog/what-is-a-network-security-assessment-whats-included-checklist-and-how-often-your-business-needs/)). Fusion Computing in Canada sells a fixed-fee assessment "from $3,000" CAD. That price covers a small single-site environment and about three days of work, and each extra site or on-prem server adds days at a published day rate ([Fusion Computing](https://fusioncomputing.ca/it-business-assessment/)). At the bottom of the market, a freelancer floor of about **$500** appears on Thumbtack ([Thumbtack Toledo](https://www.thumbtack.com/-Toledo-OH/service/2178512)).

The market rarely publishes a hard device cap. Sellers scope by endpoints per site, number of sites, on-prem servers or assessment days. Akimbo's "one site, up to N devices" is unusually explicit, and its cap counts infrastructure devices (switches, access points, firewalls, servers, hypervisor hosts), not user laptops. A 60-person office might have only 15–30 such devices. The site needs to spell out that distinction so a buyer does not read "25 devices" as "25 PCs".

**Published policy on crediting the audit fee is close to nonexistent.** The only explicit policy we found says the fee is *not* credited against any resulting agreement ([Fusion Computing](https://fusioncomputing.ca/it-business-assessment/)). Most MSPs sidestep the question by making the assessment free, or by folding it into a paid onboarding fee. That fee is quoted nationally at **$1,000–$5,000** and "covers initial setup, network assessment, and getting all your systems under management" ([Aalpha](https://www.aalpha.net/blog/how-much-does-managed-it-services-cost/)). Net Friends, a Durham firm with a Winston-Salem page, charges onboarding of "about 1 month's worth of services" ([Net Friends](https://www.netfriends.com/pricing-netvisor-managed-it)). A paid audit that is credited back therefore doubles as Akimbo's onboarding fee, and local rivals neither publish nor match that structure.

## Local per-user prices include help desk, so per-site pricing is the honest comparison

The Triad's visible plan prices all describe full-stack managed IT. **Trinity Solutions says managed IT "typically ranges" $100–$300/user/month, about $2,000–$5,000/month for a 20-person office.** Plans are custom-built after a free assessment, with no network or server line items ([Trinity Solutions Winston-Salem](https://trinitysolutionsinc.com/managed-it-services-in-winston-salem/)). Petronella publishes three per-seat tiers, Essential for 5–25 users, Business for 25–100 and Enterprise for 100–250, but shows no dollar amounts; Essential reads only "Starting at a per-seat monthly rate" ([Petronella managed IT plans](https://petronellatech.com/it-services/managed-it-services-plans/)). Its Greensboro guide tells a 30-person company to budget **$3,000–$6,000/month** ([Petronella Greensboro guide](https://petronellatech.com/blog/it-services-greensboro-nc-complete-guide-managed-it-support-triad-businesses/)). IDeACOM in Winston-Salem uses a cumulative Core / Secure / Complete ladder, quoted after a consult ([IDeACOM](https://ideacomnc.com/managed-it-services/winston-salem/)). Net Friends is the only NC firm we found that prints numbers. It uses per-user bands of **$129 for 4–20 users, $70 for 21–50 and $60 for 51–100** ([Net Friends](https://www.netfriends.com/pricing-netvisor-managed-it)). National vendor guides agree with the local figures: N-able puts standard managed services at $110–$175/user, with security bundles at $200–$275 ([N-able](https://www.n-able.com/blog/msp-pricing-guide)).

None of these figures applies directly to Akimbo, because help desk and desktop support drive most of their cost. Onsite Technical Services in Phoenix prices network management as a separate per-site line. That line is **$50–$200/site/month on client-owned gear, or $150–$400 with OTS-supplied hardware**, inside a 20-user bill of about $3,050–$4,400. The network is therefore roughly 2–10% of a full managed IT invoice. OTS's rationale applies directly to Akimbo: "network infrastructure scales with locations and complexity, not user count" ([OTS](https://www.onsite-tech.com/2026/10/18/network-management-separate-line-item-managed-it/)). The best published infrastructure analog is Micro-IT, a Kentucky-area MSP, whose price list maps closely onto Akimbo's three tiers ([Micro-IT](https://micro-it.net/resources/article-it-cost)):

Per-device prices vary by an order of magnitude, from about $5 to $150 for a single network device. The spread depends on whether "managed" means alerting only or full configuration, firmware and change management with on-site response ([Innovative Inc.](https://blog.innovativeinc.net/blog/managed-it-services-cost); [EasyITGuys](https://easyitguys.com/pricing/part-3-it-pricing-deep-dive/)). Akimbo's tier names need to say which of these each tier delivers.

Opinion on publishing a "from" price is split, and the commentary on both sides is opinion rather than data. Arguments against publishing come from MSP marketers who say prices remove a reason to call ([SmarterMSP, 2020](https://smartermsp.com/keep-your-prices-off-your-msp-website/)). Technibble also argues against, but concedes that a published *minimum* screens out tire-kickers. It also warns that prices which look too low read as inexperience ([Technibble, 2022](https://www.technibble.com/advertise-msp-pricing-online/)). Arguments for publishing say "contact for pricing" turns buyers off, and that the few who publish stand out ([JumpCloud](https://jumpcloud.com/blog/msp-pricing)). We found no conversion data either way. The strongest argument for publishing is structural. Akimbo's offer is standardised and scoped by site and device, and its audit removes the uncertainty that usually justifies "call us". The main risk is anchoring. A "from $199/mo" next to local "$100–$300 per user" figures can look either suspiciously cheap or like a substitute for full IT. Copy that states the scope up front ("infrastructure only, no help desk") prevents both readings.

## Month-to-month with 30 days' notice matches the one local rival who advertises terms

**The national norm is a 1–3 year term that auto-renews, with 30–60 days' notice.** Leaving early typically costs a percentage of the remaining fees. A law-firm template recommends 50%, a 3-year term, and premiums of 20% for one-year and 25% for month-to-month contracts ([Scott & Scott LLP](https://scottandscottllp.com/termination-clauses-in-it-managed-services-contracts/)). One MSP blog reports early termination fees of "50% (or more) of the remaining contract value" and renewals that lock in unless notice is given "months in advance" ([ITS blog](https://www.itsasap.com/blog/it-contracts-exit-terms-explained)). Richard Tubb notes that "most providers ask for a 30-day notice period" ([SmarterMSP](https://smartermsp.com/how-long-should-my-msp-contracts-last/)).

In the Triad, **Trinity Solutions explicitly attacks local lock-in.** It says "Most High Point IT companies lock you into 2–3 year agreements" and "Every client can leave at the end of any month", but it states no notice period ([Trinity High Point](https://trinitysolutionsinc.com/managed-it-services-in-high-point/)). A CloudSecureTech directory snippet puts Greensboro contract lengths anywhere from month-to-month to three years ([CloudSecureTech Greensboro](https://www.cloudsecuretech.com/us/it-services/managed/greensboro/)). Net Friends also advertises "no contracts" and offsets its risk with a paid onboarding fee ([Net Friends](https://www.netfriends.com/pricing-netvisor-managed-it)). Month-to-month therefore matches the most aggressive local rival rather than beating it. Akimbo can still lead on terms by publishing what nobody local publishes: a specific notice period, no early termination fee, and a written offboarding commitment.

The offboarding clause is the clearest open gap. The main customer complaint about leaving an MSP is withheld documentation and passwords changed just before a transition ([N-able](https://www.n-able.com/blog/how-offboard-customer)). MSP-side guidance says basic handover of credentials and environment documentation belongs in the recurring fee, with extra help billed hourly and handover meetings capped at 1–2 hours ([Giant Rocketship](https://giantrocketship.com/blog/defend-yourself-from-abusive-msp-client-offboardings)). No Triad MSP we found publishes any offboarding terms.

## Business hours and on-site within a radius are the local tier levers

Small-business plans locally and nationally staff support during business hours and add 24/7 automated monitoring. IT Solutions, a national MSP with a Winston-Salem page, publishes the most concrete local service-level document. Its help desk is staffed weekdays **7 AM–7 PM Eastern**, with 24/7 monitoring that pages on-call engineers within 15 minutes. Alerts after 11 PM wait until 7 AM, and the document states plainly that it "cannot provide a guaranteed resolution time" ([IT Solutions SLO PDF](http://www.itsolutions-inc.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/07/IT-Solutions-%E2%80%93-Service-Level-Objectives-%E2%80%93-Managed-IT-Services.pdf)). Trinity claims response targets of **15 minutes for critical issues, 1 hour for high priority and 4 business hours for routine requests** ([Clutch: Trinity](https://clutch.co/profile/trinity-solutions)). Net Friends guarantees a 30-minute response on critical issues, 24x7 ([Net Friends](https://www.netfriends.com/pricing-netvisor-managed-it)). Both are multi-staff shops. National tier ladders tighten response targets and add after-hours cover as the tier rises. N-able's pattern is business hours, then after-hours, then a 24/7 SOC ([N-able](https://www.n-able.com/blog/msp-pricing-guide)). Huntress's is remote, then on-site visits, then 24/7 emergency support, with no more than 3–4 tiers ([Huntress](https://www.huntress.com/msp-guide/building-your-msp-pricing-model)). Ad hoc after-hours work is commonly billed at **1.25–1.5x when scheduled and 2x for emergencies** ([NetFire](https://netfire.com/support-center/guides/tech-market/managed-technology/fee-schedule); [EasyITGuys](https://easyitguys.com/docs/hourly-support-rates/)).

On-site policy is where Triad buyers have the strongest expectations. Trinity advertises **"No trip charges. No fine print."** and same-day on-site visits ([Trinity High Point](https://trinitysolutionsinc.com/managed-it-services-in-high-point/)). Petronella claims "unlimited remote and on-site support with no per-ticket fees" ([Petronella](https://petronellatech.com/managed-it-services-winston-salem-nc/)). IDeACOM keeps on-site hours for its top tier ([IDeACOM](https://ideacomnc.com/managed-it-services/winston-salem/)). Net Friends includes on-site within 50 miles of Durham and charges a travel surcharge beyond that ([Net Friends](https://www.netfriends.com/pricing-netvisor-managed-it)). Micro-IT's 1 / 3 / 6 included on-site hours per month is the only published per-tier allowance we found ([Micro-IT](https://micro-it.net/resources/article-it-cost)). A visible trip fee inside Forsyth and Guilford counties would read poorly against these claims. "Unlimited", on the other hand, is a promise a solo operator cannot safely make.

## A $150 hourly anchor sits about 3x a Triad sysadmin's wage

**The only Triad firm we found with published hourly rates is Piedmont Technical Experts in Greensboro: $125/hr remote and $150/hr on-site**, with a $75 on-site diagnostic within the Triad and mileage charged outside it. It labels these as residential rates and says "contracts have better rates" ([Piedmont Technical Experts](https://piedmonttechnicalexperts.com/it-services-greensboro-nc/price-list/)). Navarac Technical Solutions in Winston-Salem lists **$150–$199/hr** and a $1,000 minimum project on Clutch ([Clutch: Navarac](https://clutch.co/profile/navarac-technical-solutions)). Charlotte's AT-NET lists the same $150–$199 band ([Clutch Charlotte](https://clutch.co/it-services/msp/charlotte)). Greensboro cost-guide aggregators, which disclose no method, estimate $100–$200/hr on-site and $75–$150 remote ([AreaCosts Greensboro](https://areacosts.com/greensboro/it-support/)). National figures run higher at the top. Project engineers bill $125–$250/hr, and scheduled on-site work costs $150–$250/hr with a 2-hour minimum. Prepaid hour blocks of 20, 40 or 80 hours typically carry a 10–20% discount ([Managed Services Pricing Guide 2025](https://managedservicesnearyou.com/pricing-guide), unsourced).

Local wages support those rates with margin to spare. In BLS OEWS May 2025 data, network and computer systems administrators earn a mean of **$48.42/hr in Winston-Salem and $44.87/hr in Greensboro–High Point**, against $49.85 nationally and $51.48 in Raleigh. Computer user support specialists earn $29.34 and $28.02/hr, against $32.37 nationally ([BLS Winston-Salem](https://www.bls.gov/oes/current/oes_49180.htm); [BLS Greensboro–High Point](https://www.bls.gov/oes/current/oes_24660.htm)). At a fully loaded cost of about 1.3–1.4x wage and 60–70% utilisation, break-even for sysadmin-level work is roughly $90–$110/hr. That leaves $150–$195 comfortably profitable while staying mid-market.

No local project prices were published, so quoting anchors are national. A commercial Cat6 drop runs about **$150–$300** installed, against an IBISWorld 2026 benchmark of $197 ([IBISWorld](https://www.ibisworld.com/united-states/procurement/network-cable-installation/48688842/); [Unio Digital](https://unio.digital/blog/structured-cabling-cost-benchmark)). The nearest regional figure is $125–$250 per drop in Greer, SC ([Swivl](https://swivl.tech/cost-guide/structured-cabling/greer-sc)). Access point installation labour runs $200–$500 per AP ([The Network Installers](https://thenetworkinstallers.com/blog/how-much-does-wifi-installation-cost/)). Firewall hardware for 15–100 users costs $1,500–$4,000 ([Fortinet](https://www.fortinet.com/uk/products/network-firewall-pricing)). An SMB network refresh runs $5,000–$20,000 ([Managed Services Pricing Guide 2025](https://managedservicesnearyou.com/pricing-guide)). We found no published price for an on-prem virtualisation build such as a single Hyper-V or Proxmox host.

The competitive field is crowded with generalists and empty of specialists. Dynamic Quest (Greensboro, now multi-state), Beacon IT Services, First Column, Trinity, Total Computer Solutions, Mindcore and a CMIT franchise all sell full-stack managed IT and security. Petronella and Net Friends reach in from the Triangle ([Dynamic Quest](https://dynamicquest.com/blog/largest-computer-services-provider-in-the-triad/); [Beacon](https://www.beacontechnologies.com/beacon-it-services/managed-it-services/); [First Column](https://firstcolumn.com/area-served/greensboro-nc)). **None we found positions as infrastructure-only.** Beacon already markets co-managed IT, which suggests generalist MSPs that need a network and server specialist are a second possible channel. Census County Business Patterns for 2023 count **2,575 establishments with 10–99 employees in Forsyth County and 4,025 in Guilford**, about 6,600 in the two core counties and 9,887 across nine Triad counties ([Census CBP 2023](https://www2.census.gov/programs-surveys/cbp/datasets/2023/cbp23co.zip)). These are locations rather than independent firms, so the addressable market is smaller, plausibly a few thousand.

## Conclusion

The research changes the framing of issue 14. The question is less "what do Triad MSPs charge" than "what is Akimbo's price *against*". Local buyers see free audits and per-user prices quoted on request, and that per-user price bundles in a help desk Akimbo does not sell. A fair local comparison does not exist, so the site has to create one. The clearest way is to price per site and per server, state the scope plainly, and make the paid audit visibly worth more than a free scan. Crediting the audit fee turns it from a hurdle into a deposit. That one design choice answers the "why pay when others are free" objection and also covers onboarding cost, which is the industry's usual reason for lock-in.

The weakest points in the evidence deserve a quick check before launch. The comparables for infrastructure-only plans are all outside North Carolina, and Triad audit pricing is unobserved because every local rival gives the audit away. Five or six mystery-shopper calls to Trinity, ComTech, Infranet, Beacon, First Column and CMIT Greensboro, asking what a network-only engagement and a paid assessment would cost, would test the numbers above more cheaply than any further desk research. The co-managed channel is worth testing in the same calls: generalist MSPs that lack server and network depth may become Akimbo's customers rather than its competitors.
