# Publish the prices Triad competitors hide

No Triad MSP publishes the things issue 14 asks Akimbo to publish. Every local firm found either gives its network assessment away, usually as a 30-60 minute check, or says "contact us". About 84% of 31 Triad-serving MSPs show no prices at all. None publishes an infrastructure-only plan, a notice period, an after-hours rate or an on-site radius. So most price benchmarks have to come from outside the region. Paid single-site audits list at **$750-$3,500** nationally, with **$1,495** the price that keeps recurring. Infrastructure-only MSPs that publish rate cards charge **$125-$650 per site per month** plus **$149-$325 per server**. Modelled for a 10-100 staff business, that comes to roughly **$300-$2,300 a month**, about 10-25% of the full-service bills Triad buyers are used to seeing: **$100-$300 per user**, or **$2,000-$5,000 a month for a 20-person office**. The Triad evidence on terms is clearer. "Month-to-month" is already a common local marketing claim, on-site visits without trip charges are the local norm, and business-grade hourly work bills at **$125-$200/hr** (Dynamic Quest's out-of-scope engineer rate is **$175/hr**). Akimbo's opening is transparency. It can offer a fixed-price audit that is visibly deeper than the free local checks and credited against a plan, publish "from" prices per site with the infrastructure-only scope spelled out, and publish its exit terms. The recommended starting values below are inferences sized for one engineer:

- a **$1,250 Network Audit** covering one site and up to 25 infrastructure devices, credited in full on signing within 60 days;
- plans **from $175, $395 and $575 a month**;
- month-to-month terms, **30 days' notice** and no termination fee;
- **Monday-Friday 8-5 support with 24/7 monitoring**, with response objectives counted in business hours rather than the 15-30 minute guarantees advertised by local rivals with larger teams.

## Triad firms give audits away, so a paid audit must look deeper

**Triad evidence.** In the Triad, the network assessment is a free sales tool rather than a product. **No Triad-based firm publishes a paid assessment price.**

- **Integrated Technologies** (Greensboro, High Point and Winston-Salem) starts with "a complimentary network assessment" ([Integrated Technologies](https://www.integratedtech.net/greensboro-it-support/)).
- **RTG Solutions** (Greensboro) runs a free on-site review of **about 30-60 minutes**. It produces a green/yellow/red summary, a 15-minute follow-up and a proposal the client "can take … to any other provider" ([RTG Solutions](https://rtgsolutions.tech/how-it-works/)).
- **Trinity Solutions** (High Point) offers a free assessment with a "plain-English report" and estimates of what it would cost to close the gaps ([Trinity Solutions](https://trinitysolutionsinc.com/managed-it-services-in-winston-salem/)).
- **Solace IT** (Kernersville) and **Synergy Security & Technology** (Winston-Salem) advertise free assessments or audits with no stated scope ([Solace IT](https://solaceits.com/); [Synergy S&T](https://www.synergyst.us/)).
- **Petronella** (Raleigh) does the same on its Triad landing pages ([Petronella W-S](https://petronellatech.com/managed-it-services-winston-salem-nc/)).
- **Piedmont Technical Experts and Dynamic Quest** (both Greensboro) sell network and security assessments without calling them free, and publish no price ([PTE assessments](https://piedmonttechnicalexperts.com/it-services-greensboro-nc/it-cybersecurity/network-and-vulnerability-assessments/); [Dynamic Quest](https://dynamicquest.com/services/security-assessment/)).

The only local price signals are hourly. Piedmont Technical Experts' residential list shows **$125/hr remote, $150/hr on-site, and $75 for a 30-minute on-site diagnostic within the Triad** ([PTE price list](https://piedmonttechnicalexperts.com/it-services-greensboro-nc/price-list/)).

**Outside the Triad.** Published prices for a paid single-site small-business audit cluster between **$750 and $3,500**:

**How sellers limit scope.** The national sellers draw scope lines in three ways:

- **Sites:** the base price almost always covers one site, with an extra charge for each additional site.
- **Device counts:** endpoint bands (Sequentur's under-25 tier) or explicit counts, such as OC Security Audit's ten computers, one server and three network devices.
- **On-site time:** capped at anything from Solvitech's half day to the 1-2 days quoted by NetSafe in Charlotte ([NetSafe](https://netsafesolutions.com/network-assessment-services)).

Remediation is excluded everywhere.

**What a real assessment contains.** Sequentur's test separates real assessments from sales scans: a current **network diagram**, a **complete device inventory with models, firmware, end-of-life dates and support status**, a severity-ranked findings list with effort estimates, and a 12-24 month roadmap. Sequentur also tells buyers to budget **3-10x the assessment cost for remediation** ([Sequentur](https://www.sequentur.com/what-is-a-network-assessment-and-why-your-business-should-have-one)). For a solo founder, that ratio matters more than the audit fee: the project work the audit produces is the real revenue.

**Scope language for Akimbo.** Most national scope limits count endpoints, but Akimbo's audit counts infrastructure. Its scope line therefore needs to define a "device" explicitly, for example firewalls, switches, access points, servers, hypervisor hosts and storage, to prevent disputes.

**Crediting the fee.** National practice is split, and no Triad precedent exists because Triad firms charge nothing:

- **Isofinite** credits its $1,495 fee "toward the first 3 months of a Premium or Compliance plan if you sign within 90 days of delivery" ([Isofinite](https://isofinite.com/)).
- **Cavelo**, an assessment-tool vendor, says "many MSPs credit any assessment fee toward a signed contract", but offers no prevalence data ([Security Boulevard / Cavelo](https://securityboulevard.com/2026/11/the-complete-guide-to-msp-assessment-recurring-revenue-cavelo/)).
- **Fusion Computing** (Canada) explicitly refuses credits, so that the assessment "stands as paid, independent work" ([Fusion Computing](https://fusioncomputing.ca/it-business-assessment/)).

Both positions are defensible. Crediting signals that the audit is the first step toward a plan. Not crediting signals independent advice.

**Price arithmetic.** These are calculations, not published figures. A one-day on-site audit plus report writing is about 8-12 billable hours, which at local rates of $125-$150/hr comes to **$1,000-$1,800**. Applying SerenIT's observation that smaller markets run **15-30% below** major-metro consulting rates turns the national $1,500-$3,000 single-office band into **$1,050-$2,550** ([SerenIT](https://serenitllc.com/blog/it-consultant-cost)). Because every local competitor offers its audit for free, the low end of that range is the realistic place to start.

## Infrastructure-only plans price at a fraction of the per-user bills Triad buyers see

**Triad evidence.** Local price opacity is close to total. Of about 31 Triad-based or Triad-serving MSPs checked:

- about **26 (84%) publish no figures**;
- about **4 publish only a "typical market" range**;
- **one** publishes its own plan prices. That is Greensboro IT Solutions, with $99/$199/$299/$499 tiers, but its unit is ambiguous and its copy reads as templated SEO text, so its prices are unreliable ([Greensboro IT Solutions](https://www.manageditservicesgreensboro.com/it-support-for-small-businesses/)).

Two local firms have dedicated pricing pages that explain why they show no numbers. Triad Cyber Solutions argues that a published tier "would either overcharge a simple office network or underprotect a regulated multi-site business" ([Triad Cyber Solutions](https://triadcybersolutions.com/pricing)). Preferred Data in High Point quotes per user after discovery, covers servers, firewalls, switches and access points "at your plan level", and includes **2, 4 or 8 technician hours a month** by tier ([Preferred Data](https://www.preferreddata.com/pricing)). The numbers Triad buyers do see all describe help-desk-inclusive, per-user managed IT:

- **Trinity Solutions:** **$100-$300 per user per month**, or "$2,000-$5,000 per month" for a 20-person office ([Trinity Solutions](https://trinitysolutionsinc.com/managed-it-services-in-winston-salem/)).
- **Partners Consulting Group:** $100-$250 per user "in the Piedmont Triad, Raleigh-Durham, and Charlotte areas" ([Partners CG](https://partnerscg.com/blog/managed-it-cost-small-business-north-carolina/)).
- **A lead-generation directory** (low reliability): $100-$185 per user, and $3,200-$8,000 a month for 15-50 users ([bestitmsps Winston-Salem](https://www.bestitmsps.com/us/managed-it/winston-salem/)).

The only local network-management price found is Greensboro IT Solutions' Meraki line: **$1,500 setup plus $150 a month**.

**Outside the Triad: per-user managed IT.** Full managed IT averages **$145 per user per month** (typical range $110-$185) in a 2026 directory survey of 412 MSPs and buyers. The same survey puts co-managed IT at about $85 ([bestitmsps 2026 benchmark](https://www.bestitmsps.com/reports/2026-msp-pricing-benchmark/)). VC3 prices alert-only "server and network monitoring" at **$30-$50 per user** ([VC3](https://www.vc3.com/blog/managed-it-services-cost-pricing)). Per-user pricing badly overstates infrastructure work, though. Every infrastructure-focused MSP found that publishes a rate card charges per site plus per server:

| Provider | Region | Network, per site per month | Servers and virtualisation, per month |
|---|---|---|---|
| Greensboro IT Solutions ([source](https://www.manageditservicesgreensboro.com/it-support-for-small-businesses/)) | **Triad** (low reliability) | Meraki: $150 + $1,500 setup | n/a |
| ICTAlly ([source](https://ictally.com/services/small-business-network)) | Nashville, TN | **$125** + $500 install (firewall, 1 AP, 8-port switch) | n/a |

**What these rate cards imply.** Server rates converge on about **$150-$250 a month**. Simple single-site network management starts near **$125-$150**. Micro-IT differentiates its site tiers by included on-site hours, a pattern that suits a local founder.

**Modelled totals.** The research priced three hypothetical offices on these rate cards. These are calculations, not quotes:

| Office | Equipment | Modelled monthly total |
|---|---|---|
| ~15 staff, one site | Firewall, two switches, four APs, one physical server | **$300-$480** |
| ~40 staff, one site | Thirteen network devices, one hypervisor host, three VMs | **$480-$950** |
| ~90 staff, two sites | Two hosts, eight VMs | **$1,100-$2,300** |

Those totals are roughly **10-25%** of full managed IT for the same headcount. That is why a low "from" price is credible, and also why it is dangerous: a buyer anchored on $2,000-$5,000 for 20 people could easily read a $175 plan as a cheap substitute for full IT support.

**Publish a price or quote after the audit?** There is no MSP-specific conversion data either way.

- **Against publishing:** older MSP-marketing voices argue that "a posted price gives prospects less of a reason to pick up the phone" ([SmarterMSP](https://smartermsp.com/keep-your-prices-off-your-msp-website/)).
- **For starting prices:** newer MSP-written pieces recommend publishing "price ranges or starting prices… and offer a consultation" ([NeverBlue IT](https://www.neverblueit.com/why-msps-dont-show-pricing-on-their-website-and-why-it-matters-to-post-prices/)).
- **Buyer surveys:** general B2B surveys consistently favour transparency. Pricing transparency is the **number-one change** B2B tech buyers want from vendors, at 45% ([eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/b2b-tech-buyers-crave-pricing-transparency)), and 25% of B2B buyers rank cost information as the most important element of a services website ([Clutch](https://clutch.co/press-releases/b2b-buyers-most-concerned-about-transparent-costs-b2b-companies-websites)).

Triad Cyber's objection is the strongest case against publishing. It holds for full-stack, compliance-heavy work. It is weakest for a standardised offer that scales by site, device and server, which is exactly the case where QuantComps, Micro-IT and ICTAlly already publish everything.

## Month-to-month is already a Triad cliché; published exit terms are not

**Triad evidence.** No-lock-in claims are common locally, but none comes with mechanics.

- **Trinity Solutions** says "every client can leave at the end of any month" and claims that "most Winston-Salem IT companies lock you into 2–3 year agreements" ([Trinity Solutions](https://trinitysolutionsinc.com/managed-it-services-in-winston-salem/)).
- **ComTech** (Graham/Greensboro), which explicitly targets 10-100 employee firms, told MSP Success in January 2025 that it runs month-to-month agreements on two per-seat tiers ([Jumpfactor](https://www.jumpfactor.net/top-managed-it-services-greensboro/); [MSP Success](https://mspsuccess.com/cracking-the-code-what-survey-results-reveal-about-msp-pricing-and-profitability/)).
- **ITECH2** advertises "No contracts" and a 30-day free trial on its Winston-Salem page ([ITECH2](https://itech2.net/city/winston-salem/)). SpectrumWise, Insight Tech Advisors and Winston IT make similar claims on Triad pages.
- **Dynamic Quest**, the largest locally headquartered MSP, uses a non-public master agreement plus service addenda and invoices "one (1) month in advance" ([Dynamic Quest Addendum D](https://info.dynamicquest.com/hubfs/Service%20Description%20PDFs/Addendum%20D%20-%20Trouble%20Ticket%20Incident%20Reporting%20-%2020Mar2025.pdf)).

**No Triad firm publishes a notice period, an early-termination rule or a price-change clause.** So month-to-month on its own does not differentiate Akimbo. Publishing the exact mechanics would.

**Outside the Triad.** The default still favours lock-in. In the MSP Success reader survey, **60% of MSPs offer one-year terms, 54% three-year and 38% monthly**, and 45% give no discount for longer commitments. Respondents could pick several options, and no sample size is reported ([MSP Success](https://mspsuccess.com/cracking-the-code-what-survey-results-reveal-about-msp-pricing-and-profitability/)). Scott & Scott sells a law-firm contract template to MSPs, and it is the likely model for many local agreements. It sets:

- automatic 12-month renewal unless written notice arrives **at least 60 days** before expiry, with renewals on "then-current terms";
- an early-exit fee of **50% of the remaining monthly fees** plus any discounts given;
- a floor that stops the client reducing seats below the first invoice;
- the provider's own right to walk away on 30 days' notice ([Scott & Scott template](https://scottandscottllp.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/MSP-Essentials-Service-Attachment-for-Managed-Services.pdf)).

Bonelli Systems in Dallas is stricter still: 36 months standard, no month-to-month option, and 50% of remaining fees to leave early ([Bonelli Systems](https://bonellisystems.com/our-service-agreement/)). At the other end, Networks Unlimited runs an open-ended agreement terminable on **30 days' written notice** with no exit fee. It also lets clients leave penalty-free if fees rise more than 10% in a year ([Networks Unlimited MSA](https://networksunlimited.com/msa/)). A 2026 buyer guide calls **"90+ days' written notice" a red flag**, treats **30-60 days** as fair, and calls a fee equal to the full remaining contract value a red flag too ([MSP Companies](https://mspcompanies.us/blog/msp-contract-red-flags)).

**The case for lock-in.** MSP-side advisers argue that month-to-month carries higher cost and churn risk. One law firm recommended adding 25% to month-to-month prices ([Scott & Scott article](https://scottandscottllp.com/termination-clauses-in-it-managed-services-contracts/); [Kaseya](https://www.kaseya.com/blog/why-msps-should-revisit-contract-terms-before-going-month-to-month/)). For a solo founder, revenue volatility is a real concern. But Akimbo has low fixed costs and a no-lock-in message to protect, and onboarding cost can be recovered without lock-in. Net Friends in Durham charges a one-time onboarding fee of "about 1 month's worth of services" ([Net Friends](https://www.netfriends.com/pricing-netvisor-managed-it)). A paid audit does the same job.

**Exit terms.** The sharpest contrast is in what happens when a customer leaves.

- **Dynamic Quest** offers customers access to their own IT Glue documentation only as a "monthly recurring charge". The access is usually read-only, and Dynamic Quest "has the right to … refuse" it ([Dynamic Quest Customer Access Tools](https://info.dynamicquest.com/hubfs/Service%20Description%20PDFs/Customer%20Access%20DQ%20Tools%20-%208Jun2025.pdf)).
- **The Scott & Scott template** requires the provider to hold "exclusive administrative credentials". It bills transition help at prevailing rates, and only if termination fees are paid. It charges a "data-copy fee" and deletes client data after 30 days.
- **Buyer guides** now tell customers to demand "all documentation, credentials, backups and configurations" within a defined 30-60 day transition, and their data within ten business days ([MSP Companies](https://mspcompanies.us/blog/msp-contract-red-flags)).

No Triad competitor's page mentions offboarding at all. Akimbo already shows Customer infrastructure from NetBox in a Client Portal, so customer-owned documentation is something it has to begin with, not a cost it would add.

## One engineer can promise business hours, monitoring and next-day on-site, not 15-minute response

**Triad support hours and response times.** The detailed local commitments come from multi-staff shops.

- **Dynamic Quest** promises "24x7x365 support" with severity-based response *objectives* ([Dynamic Quest Addendum D](https://info.dynamicquest.com/hubfs/Service%20Description%20PDFs/Addendum%20D%20-%20Trouble%20Ticket%20Incident%20Reporting%20-%2020Mar2025.pdf)). They are **30 minutes** for a majority-of-users outage (2-hour resolution), **90 minutes** for 10-49% of users, and **4 business hours** for 5-10% of users.
- **Trinity's** Clutch profile claims **15 minutes** for critical issues, 1 hour for high priority and 4 business hours for routine requests. Its website promises "local help desk during business hours, 24/7 system monitoring" ([Clutch: Trinity](https://clutch.co/profile/trinity-solutions); [Trinity Solutions](https://trinitysolutionsinc.com/managed-it-services-in-winston-salem/)).
- **Piedmont Technical Experts** lists **Monday-Friday 8:00-5:00** and no weekend cover ([PTE price list](https://piedmonttechnicalexperts.com/it-services-greensboro-nc/price-list/)).
- **IT Solutions**, a national MSP with a Winston-Salem page, staffs its desk weekdays 7am-7pm Eastern. Alerts arriving after 11pm are answered at 7am, and it states it "cannot provide a guaranteed resolution time" ([IT Solutions SLO](http://www.itsolutions-inc.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/07/IT-Solutions-%E2%80%93-Service-Level-Objectives-%E2%80%93-Managed-IT-Services.pdf)).
- **The only Triad after-hours price found** is a Winston-Salem owner-operator's **$125/hr emergency rate against a $95/hr business rate**, about 1.3x ([Great Scott Services](http://www.greatscottservice.com/services.html)).

Elsewhere in NC, Net Friends in Durham guarantees a 30-minute critical response around the clock, from a much larger team ([Net Friends](https://www.netfriends.com/pricing-netvisor-managed-it)).

**Outside the Triad.** The models closest to a solo operator are explicit about their limits. Bonelli Systems works "Monday through Friday, 8:00 a.m. to 5:00 p.m.", has "no 24/7 IT help desk", and says "response targets are targets, not guarantees" ([Bonelli Systems](https://bonellisystems.com/our-service-agreement/)). The Scott & Scott template reserves after-hours support for "critical systems outages" and allows extra charges for it. The usual after-hours premium is **1.5x with a one-hour minimum, and 2x on holidays** ([Networks Unlimited MSA](https://networksunlimited.com/msa/); [IT Glue](https://www.itglue.com/blog/msps-after-hours-support/)). IT Glue also notes that small MSPs can hand after-hours tickets to partner MSPs or a network operations centre. NOCDOC is a vendor of after-hours monitoring cover, so it has an interest in this conclusion, but its point is a fair one: "a single on-call technician is not a coverage model; it is a breach risk" ([NOCDOC](https://nocdoc.com/2026/05/06/it-sla-best-practices-how-to-set-measure-and-consistently-hit-service-commitments/)).

**Triad on-site norms.** The local norm is generous and mostly uncapped.

- **Trinity:** "We never charge trip fees". It offers same-day visits when needed and free return visits within five days if a fault recurs ([Trinity Solutions](https://trinitysolutionsinc.com/managed-it-services-in-winston-salem/)).
- **Petronella and ITECH2** both list on-site support as included, Petronella as "unlimited" ([Petronella W-S](https://petronellatech.com/managed-it-services-winston-salem-nc/); [ITECH2](https://itech2.net/city/winston-salem/)).
- **IDeACOM** is the exception: it reserves "on-site hours" for its top "Complete" tier ([IDeACOM](https://ideacomnc.com/managed-it-services/winston-salem/)).
- **Service radii:** Solace IT limits on-site work to a **25-mile radius** ([Solace IT](https://solaceits.com/)). Net Friends includes on-site within **50 miles** of Durham and charges a surcharge beyond that.
- **Cancellations:** Dynamic Quest makes customers cancel scheduled visits **48 hours** ahead or pay for them ([Dynamic Quest Co-Manage addendum](https://info.dynamicquest.com/hubfs/Service%20Description%20PDFs/Network%20Monitoring%20with%20Optional%20Support%20-%20Co-Manage%20Service%20-%208Jun2025.pdf)).

**Outside the Triad: on-site allowances.** National contracts usually tie on-site visits to a rule or an allowance:

- **Remote first:** on-site support only when the provider determines a fault cannot be fixed remotely, with the allowance set in each order (Scott & Scott).
- **Hours per tier:** included on-site hours that rise with the tier, as in Micro-IT's 1/3/6 hours.
- **Scheduled blocks:** prepaid eight-hour "focus days" ([E-N Computers](https://www.encomputers.com/2023/07/managed-it-services-pricing/)).

**What one engineer can sustain.** Copying Trinity's 15 minutes or Dynamic Quest's 30 minutes would set Akimbo up to fail on its worst day: two sites down at once, or the founder mid-audit across town. Infrastructure-only scope helps, because without a help desk there is no daily flood of password resets. Network and server faults are rarer but more severe. The defensible pattern:

- **Business hours:** Monday-Friday 8-5 Eastern, with response objectives on a business-hours clock, measured from when the engineer engages, not from an automated acknowledgement, and stated as targets.
- **After hours:** best-effort after-hours response for critical outages in the upper tiers.
- **On-site:** included when a covered fault cannot be fixed remotely, with a next-business-day target and same-day visits when the calendar allows.
- **Backup cover:** a named backup arrangement for holidays and illness.

The Winston-Salem median rose about 12% from $40.56 in May 2023 ([BLS May 2023](https://www.bls.gov/oes/2023/May/oes_49180.htm)), which supports annual rate reviews. A $150/hr bill rate is 3.3x the local sysadmin median. That sits between Piedmont Technical Experts' $150 on-site list rate and Dynamic Quest's $175, and well above the solo break-fix shops prospects might compare it with.

**Project benchmarks.** No Triad firm publishes project prices, so every anchor here comes from elsewhere:

- **Cabling:** Cat6 drops cost **$125-$250** including materials ([Fixr](https://www.fixr.com/costs/upgrading-to-cat6-cabling), snippet).
- **Wireless:** a fully installed access point costs **$650-$1,400**, and a 15-50 employee office Wi-Fi refresh **$8,000-$15,000** ([Netfor](https://www.netfor.com/resource-center/blog/business-wifi-installation/)).
- **Firewalls:** a managed-firewall installation costs **$1,400-$2,000** one-time, in a 2022 article ([VC3 firewall](https://www.vc3.com/blog/managed-firewall-cost)).
- **Hypervisor migration:** SMB labour runs **$3,000-$15,000**, a non-US vendor estimate seen only as a snippet ([Softline](https://www.softline.kiev.ua/en/news/en-hypervisor-choice-in-2026-migration-costs-and-business-value/)).
- **Block-hour discounts:** these run only **3-8%** ([Connecting Point](https://cpointcc.com/?p=3534), snippet). The 2023 survey shows a 4% gap between block and hourly rates.
- **State reference:** North Carolina's own IT department charges agencies **$500 per site** for a LAN installation ([NCDIT](https://it.nc.gov/services/billing-rates/current-service-rates/2026-2027-network-service-rates)).

**Market size.** The addressable market is large. Census County Business Patterns counts **6,600 establishments with 10-99 employees in Forsyth and Guilford counties**, and about 8,800 across the two metro areas ([Census CBP 2023](https://www2.census.gov/programs-surveys/cbp/datasets/2023/cbp23co.zip)). That is an upper bound, because chain branches buy IT centrally.

**Supply is fragmented.** The two counties have **310 computer-systems-design establishments, about 71% of them with fewer than five employees**.

**The competitors.** The competition is full-service and quote-only:

- **Dynamic Quest** is owned by private-equity firm Spire Capital. It bought Boca Raton's NetOne Technologies in July 2025 after earlier acquisitions ([Dynamic Quest](https://dynamicquest.com/blog/dynamic-quest-acquires-netone-technologies/); [PrivSource](https://www.privsource.com/acquisitions/deal/spire-capital-acquires-dynamic-quest-ane_oMSqXo)).
- **Franchises:** CMIT Solutions and TeamLogic IT.
- **Long-tenured independents:** ComTech, which a search summary says has been acquired by Harbor IT (unverified), and Total Computer Solutions.
- **The closest network-centric rival:** Cypress Networks ([Jumpfactor](https://www.jumpfactor.net/top-managed-it-services-greensboro/)).
- **Physical-infrastructure firms:** cabling integrators such as Integrated Networks and Triad Network Systems do physical work but not managed networking.

**The open niche.** The research found **no infrastructure-only MSP in the Triad**. The many one-to-five-person IT shops are as much a potential channel, as co-managed or "smart hands" partners and backup cover, as they are competitors.

All three tiers would charge about $15 a month for each network device beyond the base allowance, start additional sites at about $150 a month, and require 48 hours' notice to cancel scheduled visits. On these numbers, a 40-person office with one virtualised host comes to about **$920 a month**, and a 90-person two-site firm about **$1,900**. Both sit inside the national rate-card range and well below the full-service bills Triad buyers know.

The research changes what the audit is for. Locally the real competitor is "free", so the audit fee is less a revenue line than a qualification filter. A prospect who pays $1,250, knowing it comes back if they sign, is serious, and the remediation work the audit uncovers is worth several times the fee. The binding constraint is the founder's hours, not price. Response and on-site promises should be set for the worst day rather than the average one, and tightened only once a backup engineer exists. The largest open uncertainty is that nearly every price anchor comes from outside the Triad. Mystery-shopping three to five local firms, starting with Piedmont Technical Experts' business rates, Dynamic Quest and Trinity, would replace national proxies with local numbers before launch.
